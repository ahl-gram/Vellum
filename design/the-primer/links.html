Every step to try, on the live site

All 81 steps' Try it links, grouped by ladder, each with what to look for; then the links that show what two rulings do together. Every link opens the live site, which deployed the round's own commit.

The Explorer

i · A world from a number

  1. Press ⚄, the dice beside the seed box.

    Look for: A whole new world is drawn, and its number fills the seed box.

    The first thing a newcomer does (ruling 16). Does a whole world in a second feel like the moment that makes a child want the next step?

  2. Type 42 in the seed box and press Draw.

    Look for: The Isle of Rahai comes back, line for line. Every chart is reproducible from the number in its margin.

  3. Type any number you like in the seed box and press Enter.

    Look for: Every number is a world, and the same number is the same world for anyone who types it.

ii · The Land

  1. On the Broadside, open type and choose archipelago.

    Look for: The isle breaks into a scatter of isles, and the chart is renamed The Rahai Archipelago.

    The first step of the Land. The chart renames itself: would a child notice?

  2. A new row has come: drag islands from few toward many, then let go.

    Look for: The sea fills with isles, and the readout says about how many. A few may touch or sink, so the count is never exact.

    This row is on screen only because the link made the world an archipelago: the step needs nothing done before it.

  3. Now sizes has come too: choose of every size.

    Look for: Some isles swell and others stay small.

    A row that comes only after a count. Is "has come too" enough to say where to look?

  4. Open climate and choose polar.

    Look for: Pine woods and open tundra, and the snow line comes down the hills. The coast stays exactly where it was.

  5. Type Iceland in the shape box, and choose it from the list.

    Look for: The land is drawn inside Iceland's outline, north up, nearly edge to edge.

  6. With Iceland still in the box, choose archipelago from type.

    Look for: The islands row steps into the held box at the foot of the Land, which says why: the outline holds the count; clear the place to free the islands.

    The step that teaches why a row waits. Read the held box's note: is it plain enough for a child?

iii · The Strand

  1. Drag sea level slowly toward more sea.

    Look for: The sea rises as you drag, and the readout says how much of the sheet is land.

    The one slider that moves the chart while you drag.

  2. Drag the coast from calm toward lobed, then let go.

    Look for: Nothing moves until you let go; then a new coast is drawn. Each step of the coast is a different world.

    Nothing moves until you let go. Do the words prepare you for the pause?

  3. On a counted archipelago the coast row runs round to ragged: drag it toward ragged, and let go.

    Look for: Every island's shore frays.

  4. With Iceland's shape on the chart, drag looseness from exact toward loose.

    Look for: The land slips its outline toward the seed's own island, and the coast row comes free.

    Its words are provisional (ruling 14 teaches it now); the drift check reds if the review renames it.

iv · The Hand

  1. Open style and choose ink.

    Look for: The same world, drawn again in pen and ink.

  2. Open theme and choose rainfall.

    Look for: The land is washed with how much rain falls where.

  3. Tick arms.

    Look for: Each realm's coat of arms is set on the chart.

  4. Tick beasts.

    Look for: A sea beast is drawn in the deep water with its name beside it: on this chart, Rauhoahai.

  5. Tick survey.

    Look for: The expedition's track is inked onto the sheet, with the ship's daily stations.

  6. Untick legend, then tick it again.

    Look for: The key in the corner is taken off, and put back.

v · The Surveyor's Glass

  1. Press Draw nearer, the + by the chart.

    Look for: The chart comes closer.

  2. Drag the chart to look about it. Or click it, and use the arrow keys.

    Look for: The sheet slides under your hand. On the keys, + and − come nearer and stand off, and 0 shows the whole sheet.

  3. Keep pressing Draw nearer until the chart is drafted anew.

    Look for: A finer survey of that ground is drawn over the chart, with one corner turned up at its top right.

    "Keep pressing until": the step names no press count, since the count moves with the window.

  4. Press Hold the sheet, the anchor.

    Look for: The Glass drafts no finer survey and takes nothing away while it is lit, so the sheet you have stays put; the caption ends held.

    Provisional: the hold's glyph and name may change at its review.

  5. Press The loupe, or hold the L key with the chart in hand.

    Look for: Move the pointer over the chart: a round lens magnifies whatever lies under it.

    Provisional, like the hold. Move the pointer over the chart once it is lit.

  6. Press Stand off to step back, or The whole sheet to see it all again.

    Look for: The chart draws back to the whole sheet.

  7. On a tablet, press The chart in hand, the pointing hand, before you pinch.

    Look for: Until it is lit, a pinch zooms the page; once it is lit, your fingers move the chart.

    A tablet step (ruling 15): on a desktop its press is hidden, and the Primer shows the step with its mark.

vi · Places on the chart

  1. Click Laukuwelua, the capital.

    Look for: Its card opens: when it was founded, the name it once had, and a word of its tongue.

    A card opens on a click; on the keyboard, Tab to a town and press Enter.

  2. On the card, press View the prospect.

    Look for: The Prospect opens: the town engraved side-on, from its own ground.

  3. Or press Take the road.

    Look for: The Wayfarer's Ribbon unrolls the road from the town.

  4. Or press Lay the prospect on the table.

    Look for: The town's prospect is laid on the Chart Table, in the Drawer.

vii · The Press

  1. Press Turn The Sheet.

    Look for: The chart turns over to its back: the docket, the surveyor's attribution and the office stamp. The press now reads Turn Back, to bring it round again.

  2. Press Take to The Print Room to take this world home.

    Look for: The Print Room opens with your world on its desk.

  3. Press Read the journal in The Reading Room to watch it live.

    Look for: The Reading Room opens on the same world.

viii · The Chart Table

  1. Draw nearer until the chart is drafted anew, then press the survey's turned-up corner.

    Look for: The survey is laid on the Chart Table, and the Drawer's tab counts it.

    The longest chain in the product, by the issue's own words; this link lands with it already surveyed.

  2. Press The Drawer at the right edge.

    Look for: The Chart Table slides out with your sheets on it. It holds up to six.

  3. Press Take the table to The Portfolio.

    Look for: The Portfolio opens with every sheet you laid, ready to take home.

  4. Your commission: a polar archipelago of about forty islands, of every size, in the nautical style with its arms. Lay one survey of it on the table.

    Look for: Then copy the address from the bar. Whoever opens it sees your world, drawn the same, line for line.

    A commission. Do it, then copy the address: with the pointing hand's key after the question mark, the copied link would carry the hand too (see the rulings below).

The Prospect a room of the Explorer

i · A town, engraved

  1. In the Explorer, click a town and press View the prospect.

    Look for: The Prospect opens on that town.

  2. Read The engraver's note.

    Look for: What the town is, a line of its story, and the key to the numbers on the plate.

  3. Type an earlier year, 300, in viewed in the year, and press Engrave.

    Look for: Laukuwelua was founded in 451, so the plate shows its ground before the town rose.

    Laukuwelua was founded in 451: an earlier year shows its ground before the town rose.

  4. Press Lay this prospect on the table.

    Look for: It joins the Chart Table, to be taken home in the Portfolio.

  5. Press Take the road from Laukuwelua in The Wayfarer's Ribbon.

    Look for: The Ribbon unrolls the road setting out from this town.

The Wayfarer's Ribbon a room of the Explorer

i · A road, unrolled

  1. In the Explorer, click a town and press Take the road.

    Look for: The road from that town is unrolled strip by strip, each one inked in after the last. With motion turned off, it is all there at once.

    The strips ink in one after another; with motion off they are all there at once. The ink-in's pace is provisional.

  2. Choose another town from bound for.

    Look for: The road to it is unrolled anew.

  3. Press Turn about.

    Look for: The road runs the other way: where you were bound, you now set out from.

  4. Press a row of the itinerary.

    Look for: The Glass leans on that stretch of the road, and the chart comes nearer there.

  5. Press prospect on a town's row.

    Look for: That town's prospect opens.

  6. Press See Homaitani in The Prospect, the road out to the road's end.

    Look for: The Prospect of the town the road is bound for.

The Portfolio a room of the Explorer

i · Gather, then take home

  1. Open the Portfolio with nothing gathered.

    Look for: A plate shows the way in three steps: at the Explorer, on the Table, in the Portfolio.

    Open it in a private window, or the table you laid earlier comes back: the Chart Table is kept on the device.

  2. In the Explorer, draw nearer until the chart is drafted anew, and press the survey's turned-up corner.

    Look for: The survey is laid on the Chart Table. Lay up to six; the Drawer says when there is no room for more.

  3. Open The Drawer and press Take the table to The Portfolio.

    Look for: Every sheet you laid comes here, drafted again from its number.

  4. Press Bring up The Next Sheet.

    Look for: The next sheet comes to the top of the pile.

  5. Press Download The Engraving.

    Look for: The sheet on top is saved as the engraving, an SVG that is exact at any size.

  6. Copy the address from the bar.

    Look for: The table rides in the address, so the link keeps the gathering.

The Writing Room

i · A world from a sentence

  1. In the founding sentence box, write "a polar archipelago of twenty islands", then press Found.

    Look for: A world is founded from your words, and the account below says what each word did.

  2. Add your own name to the end of the sentence, and press Found again.

    Look for: Your name is not on the list, so it changes only the chart number: another polar archipelago of about twenty islands.

    The moment a learner sees which words steer and which only make the chart number.

  3. Write "an island in the shape of Japan", and press Found.

    Look for: The land is drawn in Japan's outline.

  4. Press Lay on The Vegetation Plate.

    Look for: The plants are laid over the land; the press now reads Lift, to take them off.

  5. Press Explore it in The Explorer.

    Look for: Your world opens in the Explorer, its Land and Strand held by your sentence: to change them, write it anew in the Writing Room.

  6. Your commission: write a sentence for a world you would like to visit, and send its link to a friend.

    Look for: The same sentence founds the same world, forever.

The Reading Room

i · Watch a world live

  1. Press Play.

    Look for: The survey ship sails first, day by day; then the years turn, and towns rise and fall. With motion turned off on your computer, Play goes straight to the last year.

    With motion turned off on your computer, Play goes straight to the last year: the step says so.

  2. Press 2× or 4× to hurry it.

    Look for: The voyage and the years run twice or four times as fast. With motion turned off there is no pace to choose, since Play is a still frame.

    Shown only with motion on; under reduced motion there is no pace to choose.

  3. Drag The ages to any year.

    Look for: The chart shows the world as it stood that year, and the line under it tells what happened then.

  4. Read The Journal at the right.

    Look for: Each line is a day of the survey, or a year of the ages.

  5. On a road leg's row in the Journal, press Take this road once the voyage has reached it.

    Look for: It asks Leave the voyage? first; then the Wayfarer's Ribbon unrolls that road.

    Provisional: the journal's road press is awaiting its review.

  6. Type a seed and press Read, or press ⚄.

    Look for: Another world sits down to be read.

The Print Room

i · A proof on the desk

  1. Type 42 and press Pull a proof.

    Look for: The Isle of Rahai comes onto the desk as a proof.

  2. Choose ink beside it, and pull again.

    Look for: The proof is pulled again in pen and ink.

ii · The Bound Atlas

  1. Press Bind the atlas.

    Look for: In a few seconds every plate is bound from the proof: the chart in every manner, the thematic surveys, the close-ups, the prospect, the road, the banners, the chronicle and the gazetteer.

  2. In the Contents, press a thumbnail.

    Look for: The sheet on the desk turns to that plate.

  3. Press Print to save the atlas as a PDF, or Download for one file that opens offline.

    Look for: Print opens your browser's own Save as PDF, one plate to a page.

iii · Posters

  1. Choose An impression (PNG), then press Desk.

    Look for: The chart is pressed as a picture 2400 pixels wide, and saved.

  2. Choose The road from the capital from the first list; the first press now reads Ribbon: press it.

    Look for: The road from Laukuwelua is pressed as a plate of its own.

    Once the road is chosen, the first press reads Ribbon: the round's first draft said Chart, and doing the step caught it.

  3. Press Back to The Explorer.

    Look for: The Explorer opens on the same world.

The Seed of the Day

i · The Daily Hunt

  1. Read the survey notes on The Daily Hunt.

    Look for: A few lines describe one forgotten place on today's chart. Everyone hunting today looks for the same place.

  2. Click the chart where you think it hides.

    Look for: A miss leaves a sounding where you clicked, and a line tells how near you came: hot, warmer, cool or cold.

  3. When you find it, press Share your find.

    Look for: A star is stamped on the place and its story unrolls; the press sends your result, or copies it.

    Share your find appears only once the place is found.

  4. Press Draft dispatch.

    Look for: Your hunt, every sounding and the star, drawn onto the chart for you to keep.

  5. Press Open today's seed in The Explorer.

    Look for: Today's world, with every control of the Explorer. Come back tomorrow for a new one.

  2. Press any plate that catches your eye.

    Look for: Its world opens in the Explorer, at its own seed.

What two rulings do together

  1. The Print Room at the 1024 floor

    Open it in a window exactly 1024 wide and look at the style list beside Pull a proof. With a ninth item on the nav the corner narrows from 358 px to 304 px and the list cuts "antique": the build owes its widening (ruling 2).

  2. The Prospect's trail line

    Under the trail: "also reached from The Reading Room". The Primer's own trail will carry "also reached from Questions & Answers" once it is both a nav item and a child of Q & A, and the trail press must find a seat that clears this line.

  3. The pointing hand's key, today

    Today the key does nothing. Move the islands slider: the key stays in the bar while the room rewrites its address. Then press Take to The Print Room: the key does not follow (measured in all nine rooms). Copy the bar: the key goes with the link.

  4. Home's How It Works card

    Press How It Works at the title cartouche. The road to the Primer would sit at the foot of this card, under "Draw your own: read the README".

  5. The FAQ's nearest step, out of date

    "I found a map I love": its answer says type, climate and land are not saved, and the address saves them all (a sibling defect, filed separately).
