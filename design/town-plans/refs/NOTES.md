# Town plans: what the references taught the round

Issue #793's references, six period plans on Wikimedia Commons, fetched by `refs-tool/fetch-refs.ts` (in this folder's parent) into this folder as working copies. `refs.json` carries, for each, its Commons page, its original URL and pixel size, the size of the copy fetched, and its licence field as the Commons API gives it. By that file, 5 are public domain and 1 is CC BY-SA 2.0 (Palmanova, `palmanova.jpg`). The archive keeps the links, never the images.

## Streets that follow the land (direction A)

- **Cologne, Hogenberg** (`cologne.jpg`, no date on the file). The wall is a half ring on the land side, with its ditch outside. The river front on the Rhine is one continuous row of buildings. Streets run in from the land gates, and the blocks between them are irregular and packed. This is direction A's wall, standing on the land and shaped to it.
- **Bern, Merian after Plepp, 1638** (`bern.jpg`). The town is laid along the ridge inside its river's loop. Its long streets run with the ridge, the cross lanes are short, and the blocks are long and narrow. The bastions stand on the land side, across the neck of the loop. This is "follow the land" where the land has a direction.

## Streets on a grid (direction B)

- **Aigues-Mortes, Bellin, 1764** (`aigues-mortes.jpg`). A planned port: a rectangle of blocks inside a rectangular wall at the water. The strict answer, a grid inside a wall drawn to it.
- **Valletta, D. Specle, 1582 by the file's date field** (`valletta.jpg`, 650 px; the file's own name says 1589). A planned capital laid on a grid across its peninsula, with the bastioned wall following the shore. This is direction B as drawn: a grid inside a wall shaped to the ground.

## Between the two, and a third answer not drawn

- **Freiburg im Breisgau, Merian, 1644** (`freiburg.jpg`). A founded town. Its market streets cross at right angles inside a wall shaped to the ground, and the castle stands on the hill above. This is the "keep takes the height" rule, and it shows that a founded town can sit between A and B.
- **Palmanova, Hoefnagel, 1593 by the file's title** (`palmanova.jpg`, CC BY-SA 2.0; the file's date field is its upload). The ideal town: streets radiating from a central square to the gates, inside a star of bastions. It is an honest third street system, and the round does not draw it.
