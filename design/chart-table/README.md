# The Chart Table mockups (#518, Sub 0 of #401)

The visual SPEC candidates for the Chart Table epic, drawn 2026-09-06 from real seed-42 sheets in
the atelier's current dress, for the sitting that rules the five decisions #401's ruling 11 parked
here (where the table sits and its posture; full table or count-and-button; the phone; the names
and the address key; the folio page's presentation). Precedents: the atelier-map mockup (#454),
the room mockups (#462 and #494, `design/sub7-chart-rooms/`), the ribbon mockups (#511,
`design/ribbon-limner/`). **The rulings land as a dated comment on #518 and a ledger on #401**;
until then nothing here is ruled.

The guide beyond the kit was a collage artist's desk (davesocozy.com, "a table to cut your own"):
a working mat in the middle, a tray of clippings beside it, six labelled folders along the near
edge, loose paper at slight angles. Each direction lifts one piece of that desk into the atelier's
own furniture and nothing else; the deep, the faces, the slip, the legend row and the Glass are the
kit's as it stood at main `74da88b`.

## The four directions

Every direction shares the same stage (the Explorer with the survey of the environs of Laukuwelua
committed at band 2, the world sheet magnified behind it as the live page shows it) and the same
six sheets, laid in this order: the environs of Laukuwelua at band 1, the environs of Nurunui at
band 3, the prospect of Paukilua in pen and ink, the environs of Paukilua at band 2, the environs
of Stanbyl at band 2 from chart 15, and the prospect of Laukuwelua in antique. Every survey is
antique because every collectible survey is: the redraft is gated on the antique dress
(`regionEligible`), so the only other dress that can reach the table is a prospect's pen and ink.
The first three are the "three sheets filed" state #518 asks for (two surveys and an ink prospect);
the sixth sheet fills the cap. The chart-15 sheet is there so the folio page can show ruling 4
(different worlds, drafted grouped by world), which the issue as written never exercised.

**The place card's capture press** is common to every direction and drawn at `?stage=world`
(`explorer-1280-card`, `explorer-390-card`): the world sheet at band 0 with the capital's card pinned,
and beside "View the prospect" a second press in the same gold, "Lay the prospect on the table",
which dims to "No room on the table" at the cap. The thumbnail it produces is the ink prospect on
every table here.

**On a phone the handle also docks**: the inset stands above the open sheet, off the screen, so while
the sheet is open the same press rides the sheet's legend ("Lay this survey on / The Table", drawn in
every `x-explorer-390-open` still beside the table's road), in every direction.

**The handle on the inset** is common to A, B and C: a small gold press in the kit's two-line road
dress, clipped to the survey's top-right corner, reading "Lay on / The Table"; at the cap it dims
and reads "No room on / The Table". D replaces it with a dog-ear.

- **A, the tray.** A strip along the bottom edge in the Reading Room's strip idiom, showing only
  when it holds a sheet: the sheets laid lie in it as small paper slips at their own tilts, each with
  its title and a small-caps line (band, dress, and the chart number when it is another world's),
  a press to take it off, the count at the left and the road out ("Take the table to / The Print
  Room") at the right. Always showing; the room's fit reserves its height, so the chart stands
  smaller while the table holds sheets. The full table (needs the #193 exemption line). On a phone
  the strip thins to a row of thumbnails under the sheet's handle and the road docks in the sheet's
  legend.
- **B, the docket.** The #193 minimum: nothing in the room but the handle and one italic line under
  the Press row, "three sheets on the table · take the table to the Print Room", in the Print
  Room's own legend-status idiom. The sheets are first seen on the folio page. On a phone the road
  docks in the sheet's legend as a gold button.
- **C, the pigeonholes.** A rack of six holes at the left edge, mirroring the slip on the right,
  numbered i to vi: an empty hole is a dashed frame reading "lay a survey here", a filled one holds
  the sheet, and a hover names it. The cap is the furniture, so there is no refusal line: the sixth
  filled hole IS the refusal, and the handle dims. Always showing. The road out sits under the rack.
  On a phone the rack lies along the bottom edge above the sheet, six holes across, numerals only.
- **D, the drawer.** Shut at rest behind a tab on the right edge ("The Drawer · three sheets"); open,
  it slides up over the foot of the stage and the sheets lie loose on the walnut as cuttings with
  paper labels, the chart's folio, the Press and the Glass lifting above it. The handle is the
  survey's own corner turned back (the dog-ear), its label "lay this survey on the table". Slides
  out. On a phone the drawer is the sheet's second leaf: two tabs on the sheet's head, "The
  Broadside" and "The Table · 3", and the table leaf shows the cuttings two across.

## The folio page, four presentations

A second page of the Print Room (#401 ruling 2), one per direction, mixable at the sitting:

- **A, the Portfolio** (`/print-room/portfolio/`): seat d as #494 ruled it for the bound atlas, one
  sheet on the stage, the rest as thumbnails in the slip's contents rows beside their titles, each
  row carrying its own download ("the engraving (SVG)"); the rows grouped by world under a head per
  chart; two rows still drafting in #329's reserved frame.
- **B, the Chart Table** (`/print-room/table/`): the same seat with rows only, no thumbnails.
- **C, the Sheaf** (`/print-room/sheaf/`): the six as a pile on the stage, the top sheet fitted and
  the rest peeking out behind at their own tilts; the slip is the sheaf's index and a title brings
  a sheet to the top.
- **D, the Chart Table** (`/print-room/table/`): no slip; the six laid out on the deep three across
  at slight tilts with paper labels, each label carrying the download; the drafting ones as frames.

Every presentation keeps each sheet's own dress (ruling 8) and downloads each sheet on its own
(ruling 9); the corner's gloss says so, and the slip's foot says there is no bound file of the table.

The built pages open from `file://`; direction and state are query strings (`explorer.html?dir=c&state=six`,
`folio.html?dir=d`, `explorer.html?dir=d&drawer=0` for the drawer shut, `&leaf=table` on a phone
for D's table leaf, `&stage=world` for the band-0 sheet with the place card), never the hash, for the reason the sub7 README gives. `shoot.mjs` is the sub7
shooter copied here so the archive stands alone; `--window-size` does not lay out at 390, only
device metrics do. `kit.css`, `shell.css`, `explorer-page.css` and `folio-page.css` are copies of
the live sheets at `74da88b` (the shell's tokens and head cluster from BaseLayout, motion, house,
atelier; the Explorer's and the Print Room's page sheets) so the mock wears the kit as it stood;
`mock.css` is the mock's own furniture and names every deviation.

## What the recon found that the sitting should know

- Seat d was ruled on **#494** (comment 2026-08-30), not #462 as #518's body says.
- The chart folio standing down at narrow widths is a separate, untested CSS rule
  (`.corner.bl { display: none }` in atelier.css's narrow block), not the 2026-09-03 sitting's ruling
  1, which covers the Glass only.
- `chartTarget` lives in `src/site/prospect/address.ts` (and the Ribbon's twin), not the Explorer's
  address module.
- When Sub 3 makes the folio page real, it joins `CHART_ROOMS` in `test/site/atelier-kit.test.ts`
  and the AK sweeps at once.
- **A live kit defect, filed as #525 from this round**: on a phone with the camera zoomed and the
  sheet open, the zoomed footing's `::before` outranks `.legend.in-slip`'s `content: none` and paints
  the walnut pool over the parchment sheet (measured on the built site at 74da88b, the slip's ground
  reading `srgb(103,91,74)`). The Explorer shows it at any width up to 900px once a survey is
  committed; the Gallery shows it with no zoom at all; Sub 3's page would inherit it. The mock
  neutralizes it (one rule at the top of `mock.css`) so the phone stills show the sheet as the kit
  intends, and the rulings are not made from a defect.

## Known demo-only shortcuts

- The pages are static recompositions: the chart pans and zooms, the slips fold, the drawer opens
  and shuts, but the handle, the presses and Draw do nothing.
- The mock seats the sheet and the world behind it with its own script (`shared.js`); the real
  build's fit is `room.ts`'s and the world-behind is the Glass's own transform.
- The legend row's seat is the mock script's copy of `placeLegendRow` (`src/site/shared/room-seats.ts`):
  from the chart folio's text edge, capped by the chrome inset, the Glass and the open slip, its
  width capped to the space left, and seated before the fit. At 1280 with the Broadside open the
  Explorer's three roads already fill that space, so the row wraps where the live page's does.
- The phone stills carry the #525 override above; the live page at those widths does not, until #525
  lands.
