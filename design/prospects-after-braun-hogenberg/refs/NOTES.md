# Civitates Orbis Terrarum: what the plates taught, plate by plate

Issue #747, round two of the prospects. Every scan below is a public-domain or CC0 reproduction on Wikimedia Commons, downloaded by `fetch-refs.py` at a 3000 px working width into this folder (not committed). `refs.json` carries each file's Commons page, original URL, pixel size and licence field. Crops for close reading are in `crops/`.

## The conventions that recur across the set

- **The horizon is high.** Almost every plate puts the horizon in the upper third or quarter, so the town fills the middle band and the ground in front of it is a real place, not a line. Today's Vellum plate puts the horizon in the lower half and leaves the sky empty.
- **Two cameras, not one.** The set has bird's-eye plans (Cologne, Antwerp, Venice, London, Seville after Braun, Maastricht, Cairo) and oblique prospects from a rise or across water (Seville after Hoefnagel, Granada, Toledo, Genoa, Lisbon, Bergen, Stockholm, Heidelberg, Salzburg, Trier, Innsbruck). The oblique prospect from a foreground hill is the closest cousin of today's side-on plate and is where the costumed figures live.
- **The sky is engraved, never blank.** Fine horizontal burin lines graded from dense at the horizon to sparse above, with cloud banks drawn as stacked hooked strokes and the lines broken around them (Seville Hispalis, Cadiz, Bergen, Lisbon). Birds are small ticks in flocks, not single marks.
- **Hills are tonal.** A hill is a filled form whose shadow side carries hatching that follows the slope, with a second lighter set crossing it on the steepest face; the lit side is left white with a sparse dotted or stippled texture (Toledo crop, Bergen, Innsbruck). Vegetation on a hill is a scatter of tiny round tree glyphs that thins uphill (Genoa, Trier, Stockholm).
- **Buildings have a lit side and a shadow side.** Every house is a box with a gable, the wall facing away from the light hatched with close parallel lines, the roof hatched along its slope with the ridge left as a white line; windows are two or three black dashes; church towers and spires get horizontal courses. In the hand-coloured copies roofs are vermilion or brick red, roofs of churches blue-grey (Toledo, Antwerp, Heidelberg, Salzburg).
- **Walls are drawn as a continuous ribbon** with regular tower drums and gates, following the ground (Seville, Cologne, Antwerp). The shadow face is hatched and the crenellations are a row of ticks.
- **Water is horizontal line work.** Dense parallel ripple lines, denser near shores and in the foreground, with sail-shaped gaps left white around hulls; wakes are small curls (Lisbon, Genoa, Antwerp). Ships are carefully drawn in three sizes: a great ship with three masts and furled sails, a galley with oars, and small boats with one sail or none.
- **Figures stand on a rise in the foreground**, two to five of them, large (a tenth to a sixth of the plate height), dressed in the costume of the place, often seen from behind or in profile looking at the town, with a dog, a donkey or a horse (Seville Hispalis, Granada, London, Salzburg, Nonsuch, Bergen, Cologne, Maastricht, Florence under Genoa). Nonsuch shows a whole costume frieze under the view with each figure captioned.
- **The furniture is standardised.** A title in Roman capitals across the sky or in a strapwork cartouche; a longer Latin description in a second strapwork panel; the arms of the realm (crowned, often in a laurel wreath) at one top corner and the arms of the city at the other; a numbered or lettered key in a panel, listing real buildings; cardinal words (Oriens, Occidens, Septentrio, Meridies) in the corners of the view; "Cum privilegio" and the artist's name in tiny italic at the foot.
- **Hand-colouring is a small box of washes over the engraving:** green for grass and trees (two values), brick red for roofs, blue-grey for water and church roofs, ochre for ground and roads, gold and vermilion in the cartouches. The ink line still carries all the form; the colour never replaces hatching.

## The plates

1. **Seville after Hoefnagel, "Hispalis", 1593 (Rijksmuseum RP-P-1910-2128)**
   https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sevilla_met_vele_figuren_op_de_voorgrond_Sevilla_Hispalis_(titel_op_object),_RP-P-1910-2128.jpg
   The model for a foreground with figures. Horizon at a third from the top; the city a long low band of walls and roofs with the Giralda the only tall accent; the whole lower two thirds is a rolling foreground with roads, a long building, and some twenty figures: two cloaked women seen from behind, a procession of penitents on donkeys, horsemen, a dog. The sky is horizontal burin lines with a title banner and two lettered keys (A to KK, L to T) hung on a garland. Ground is hatched in long parallel sweeps that follow the fall of each slope; scrub is small hooked tufts. The compass words sit in the four corners.

2. **Cologne, "Colonia Agrippina", 1572, hand-coloured (Bodleian copy, Frans Hogenberg)**
   https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Frans_Hogenberg,_Colonia_Agrippina_(FL43895807_3941855).jpg
   The classic bird's-eye: the walled semicircle on the Rhine, every block a mass of tiny oblique houses, fields outside the wall hatched in parallel furrows, the river a band of ripple lines with small boats. Two arms at the top (the imperial eagle and the city's three crowns), a title cartouche on the river, text panels in two corners, a group of four costumed figures in the lower left, a reclining river-god personifying the Rhine lower right. Colour: green fields, blue river, red-tiled roofs, orange frame.

3. **Antwerp, "Anverpia", c. 1572, hand-coloured (MET DP326686, CC0)**
   https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Anverpia_from_Braun_and_Hogenberg%27s_Civitates_Orbis_Terrarum_MET_DP326686.jpg
   Bird's-eye with the Scheldt full of shipping; the oblique houses are drawn as rows of small gabled boxes with the shadow side hatched and roofs washed red; the bastioned wall is a continuous polygon; a numbered key (1 to 24) sits in a small panel at the bottom; the title in Roman capitals across the sky. The density of the house pattern is what makes it read as a great city.

4. **Venice, "Venetia", 1572, hand-coloured**
   https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Venetia_1572.jpg
   Bird's-eye of the lagoon with a procession of the Doge and his officials in a strapwork panel below, each figure captioned, and a three-column key of churches. Shows how a key can run to over a hundred entries and still sit in the frame.

5. **London, "Londinum Feracissimi Angliae Regni Metropolis", c. 1572 (British Museum)**
   https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Londinum_Feracissimi_Angliae_Regni_Metropolis_(BM_G,2.3).jpg
   Bird's-eye with the royal arms and the city arms in laurel wreaths at the two top corners, a strapwork title cartouche top centre, two text panels at the bottom corners, and four large costumed figures standing on a bank in the foreground, over the plan. The figures are the plate's scale and its people; the city is a texture.

6. **Lisbon, "Olisippo. Lisabona" (BnF)**
   https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Olisippo._Lisabona_-_btv1b8440098w.jpg
   Profile from the river: the whole lower third is water drawn as fine horizontal lines, with a dozen ships of three sizes; the city climbs the hills behind in oblique rows; arms top-left (crowned) and a ship roundel in a wreath top-right; a 34-entry numbered key in four lines along the foot. The water line work is the model for Vellum's harbour foreground.

7. **Granada after Hoefnagel, 1563 (hand-coloured, Bodleian copy)**
   https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Joris_Hoefnagel,_Granada_(FL167449008_2375362).jpg
   A profile from the plain: a low horizon of hills, the Alhambra on its hill to the right, the city a long band; the sky is a third of the plate, with birds, clouds and a huge strapwork cartouche carrying the key (1 to 19) flanked by two costumed figures standing on it. The foreground is a road with a dozen travellers: a muleteer with water jars, veiled women, a rider at a gallop, a woman with a jar on her head. The costume is the plate's subject as much as the town.

8. **Edinburgh, "Edenburgum, Scotiae Metropolis", c. 1574 (small hand-coloured scan)**
   https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Braun_%26_Hogenberg,_Edenburgum,_Scotiae_Metropolis_(Edinburgh)_c.1574.jpg
   The hill town: the castle rock drawn as a brown tonal mass with the castle on top, the walled town below on a ridge, four figures in the foreground, a strapwork title panel. Low resolution (764 px), kept for the composition only.

9. **Genoa (with Florence), 1572, hand-coloured (Frans Hogenberg, Bodleian)**
   https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Frans_Hogenberg,_Genua_(FL167448555_2370028).jpg
   The harbour town from the sea: the mole with its lighthouse tower, a cluster of great ships moored inside it, galleys with banners in the foreground, the city a dense band of red roofs climbing to walled green hills with named forts on the summits. Florence below: fields in the foreground hatched in rows, two figures standing on the near bank. The crop `genoa-mole-ships.jpg` shows the water lines, the furled sails and how a wake is drawn.

10. **Cadiz after Hoefnagel, 1564 (Rijksmuseum RP-P-1921-874, CC0)**
    https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gezicht_op_de_landtong_van_C%C3%A1diz_Gades_ab_occiduis_insulae_partibus_(titel_op_object),_RP-P-1921-874.jpg
    Profile across a bay with a chapel in the near ground, two pilgrims, a greyhound and a pelican as curiosities, and two inset vignettes of local life in strapwork panels below, plus a 24-entry key. The plate is willing to put a creature of the place in the foreground as a feature, which licenses Vellum's bestiary to appear.

11. **Toledo after Hoefnagel, 1566, hand-coloured (Bodleian)**
    https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Frans_Hogenberg,_Toletum_(FL167468653_2490519).jpg
    The hill town above its river in an oval cartouche frame, with the cathedral and the palace drawn separately below as elevations. The crop `toledo-hills-hatch.jpg` is the clearest lesson in hill hatching: the cliff face is long curved strokes following the fall, crossed on the darkest face, with the top left white and dotted; the river is horizontal lines; houses are oblique boxes with red roofs and hatched shadow walls.

12. **Cairo, "Cairus quae olim Babylon", hand-coloured**
    https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cairus_Quae_Olim_Babylon_Aegypt_Maxima_Urbs.jpg
    Bird's-eye with the Nile, the pyramids as hatched hills to the right, a procession of figures and riders in the lower left, a long key in a strapwork panel. Shows a title written in flowing italic script across the sky instead of capitals.

13. **Heidelberg, "Heidelberga" (hand-coloured, wide profile)**
    https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Heidelberg_Braun-Hogenberg.jpg
    A long profile from across the Neckar: the castle on its hill, the town a band of red roofs with the church spire, the bridge with its gate tower, boats on the river, the near bank a strip of green with a few trees. The closest to Vellum's current composition, and shows how much a hatched sky and a tonal hill add to it.

14. **Bergen, 1580 (after Hieronymus Scholeus, greyscale scan)**
    https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bergen_Braun_Hogenberg_1580.png
    Harbour across a bay from a rocky foreground: two figures on the rocks lower right with their backs to us, ships in the bay, the town across the water, mountains behind drawn as tonal masses with the shadow sides hatched, a lettered key (A to X) in a panel top-left, four arms across the sky with crowns, a title cartouche. This is the composition Vellum's harbour plates can honestly take, since the vantage is already from the sea.

15. **Nonsuch Palace after Hoefnagel, 1582, hand-coloured**
    https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Nonsuch_Palace_by_Joris_Hoefnagel.jpg
    A palace prospect over a costume frieze: nine English figures in a row below the view, each captioned with their rank, with a fishwife scene at the end. The upper view has a royal procession with riders and a coach on a road. The figures are drawn with heavy outer contour, hatched folds, and solid black for hats and shoes, which is what keeps them legible when small.

16. **Stockholm from the south, 1570 (hand-coloured, wide profile)**
    https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Stockholm-from-south-1570-braun-hogenberg-civitates-orbis-terrarum.jpg
    The island city across water from a green near hill with windmills on the skyline to the left, a great ship in the foreground, the town a dense cluster rising to a spire and a round tower; the hills behind are soft green washes with sparse hatching.

17. **Maastricht, "Traiectum ad Mosam", 1575, hand-coloured**
    https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Civitates_Orbis_Terrarum-Maastricht_(kleur).jpg
    A near-plan bird's-eye in pale washes with the Maas as a blue band, three arms across the top, a title cartouche lower right, and a couple in costume lower left. The washes are pale: green fields, red roofs, blue water, a yellow frame line.

18. **Trier, "Treveris", 1572, hand-coloured (wide profile)**
    https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Braun%26Hogenberg_Trier_1572.jpg
    A wide oblique prospect: the Mosel curving through the foreground, the town behind its wall, the hill behind the town stippled rather than hatched, and a text cartouche top right. Green is laid in two values (bright for the near meadows, dark for the woods).

19. **Innsbruck valley after Hoefnagel, 1590s (MET DP817444, CC0)**
    https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Innsbruck_from_the_series_Civitates_Orbis_Terrarum,_vol._V,_plate_59_MET_DP817444.jpg
    Three mountain prospects on one sheet in pure ink: the clearest example of how a crag is engraved (vertical cracks, hatching that follows the rock face, trees as rows of small round clumps on the slopes), with a lettered key panel top left and figures in the foreground of each.

20. **Salzburg, "Saltzburgk", hand-coloured**
    https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Braun_Hogenberg_Saltzburgk.jpg
    A high oblique prospect from a near hill with three costumed figures on it, the fortress on its green hill behind the town, the river crossing the middle, a 37-entry key in a strapwork panel lower left and a Latin title panel lower right, two arms in strapwork at the top. The figure group (a cleric, a lady, a gentleman in slashed hose) is a good model for a three-figure group drawn from a realm's colours.

21. **Seville after Braun, 1588, hand-coloured**
    https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Georg_Braun_%26_Frans_Hogenberg_-_Sevilla.jpg
    Bird's-eye with the Guadalquivir lined with ships, three arms in wreaths across the top, a 40-entry key along the foot, and small figure groups (a picnic, a dancing couple) in the near fields. Confirms the standard furniture set.

## What this means for Vellum's plate

- Raise the horizon and give the sky its lines and clouds: the single largest change in feel, and it costs only straight strokes.
- Hatch the shadow side of every mass and the slope of every hill; today's plate has outlines and almost no tone.
- Put people on the near ground, drawn large, from the world's own data (kind, realm colours, harbour, ruin), with a dog or a mount.
- Draw the water as graded horizontal line work with the ships sitting in white gaps.
- Hang the realm's arms in a wreath, put the title in a strapwork cartouche or a banderole, letter the key into a panel, and write the compass words in the corners.
- Name what the world already names: the sea, the river, the range, the neighbouring towns on the horizon.
- The bird's-eye is a different camera and a different composer, but it is the set's signature and the engine already holds the coast, river, road and biome data it would draw from.
