A Survey of Unbuilt Coasts

Being a chart of interactive & whimsical prospects for Vellum, an atelier of imaginary cartography; with a reckoning of waters already charted, and those still open.

Drafted at the sign of the compass rose · July MMXXVI

Plate I

The Workshop Ideas

Simulate not just the world, but the workshop and the physical object — the plate, the paper, the ink, and the hands the map passed through.

A printed 17th-century map passed through a shop of specialists: mapmaker, engraver (cutting the design in reverse into copper), printer, papermaker, and colorist, who added color by hand after printing. Plates were precious, revised, and reused for decades; late impressions from worn plates show visible cracks. Even the sea monsters were often copied from books the era considered scientifically authoritative — enchantment and education.

The Engraver's Bench

  • The map engraves itself. Animate the initial draw as a time-lapse of the burin at work: coastlines scratch in first, then rivers, hatching, and lettering last.
  • Plate view. A toggle showing the matrix: the map mirrored, white-on-burnished-copper — the incised negative the engraver cut. Sign it in period style: Vellum delin. et sculp.
  • Plate wear, impressions, and states. Each seed gets a virtual copperplate with an impression number as an explicit render parameter. Impression 1 is crisp; impression 800 shows softened hatching, plate tone, and a deterministic hairline crack. "Impression No. 214, second state." States map onto existing flags — first state without legend, second state with legend and arms — so you don't export files, you pull impressions.

The Colorist's Table

The ink chart prints uncolored; the user (or a simulated apprentice colorist of selectable skill) washes in realm tints with period pigments. The kicker: pigments age. Verdigris green really did eat through paper over centuries — add a time slider (1650 → today) and watch the map fox, yellow, and burn through.

The Paper Itself

  • Hold it to the light: hover reveals chain lines and a watermark, deterministically generated per seed from a small heraldic grammar.
  • Deckle edges; a fold crease from atlas binding; an ink ghost offset from the facing page.
  • Provenance layer: an old owner's bookplate, a dealer's pencil price, a coffee ring, a wormhole — plus a generated auction-catalog description: "Engraved chart, original hand color, minor foxing, small wormhole affecting neat line, else fine."

The Surveyor's Instruments

  • Unsurveyed regions: distant regions render lighter, dashed, conjectural — hic sunt dracones — and zooming in is the act of surveying: the survey party arrives, and fresh detail draws itself in.
  • Dividers cursor: click two ports; brass dividers walk the distance and report it in leagues.
  • Candlelight mode: a dark workshop, the map lit by a flickering candle radius, a magnifying lens following the cursor.
  • Rotatable compass rose, with an east-up option — "orienting" a map literally comes from putting the Orient at top.

The Marginalia & Lore Desk

  • A bestiary: sea serpents become clickable, each citing an invented authority in the culture's own language — "after the Thalassographia of Maro Veshel."
  • Wind heads that blow, and a tiny ship sailing a rhumb line across the chart on a slow loop.
  • The expedition journal: the lore writer drafts the survey log that "produced" the map — evidence of a voyage that never happened.
  • A trap hamlet: real cartographers planted phantom "paper towns" as copyright traps. Every seed gets one deterministic phantom hamlet, unmarked in the legend. Hint at it in the FAQ; let people hunt.
  • The dedicatory cartouche: type a name, receive a properly pompous dedication — "To the Most Illustrious ALEX, this Chart is humbly Dedicated by the Surveyor."
Status: parked. These ideas run close to work already charted — the Print Room, the Reading Room, the motion doctrine. Kept here for reference and salvage.
Plate II

The Myst Direction, Reconciled

The brief: Minecraft's generativity with the original Myst's refinement.

The design stake. Minecraft's loop is modification; Myst's is comprehension. Vellum's identity — same seed, same world, identical SVG — says comprehend. Lean Myst; borrow only Minecraft's infinity. No terrain editing: the moment users can move a mountain, determinism dies, and that sentence is Vellum's whole personality.

Open water — no ticket anywhere

Open1 · Write the Age ★

Every current entry point to a world is a number. Instead: write a founding sentence — "a cold archipelago of black pines, iron harbors, and a drowned kingdom" — and Vellum parses the descriptors it recognizes into generation parameters (band, type, land fraction, culture weights) and hashes the remainder into the seed. The sentence is engraved into the cartouche as the world's founding text. Deterministic: same sentence, same world. It turns generation into authorship, and it composes with everything built — a written age gets a voyage, a chronicle, a hunt. The one idea that changes what kind of object a Vellum world is.

Open2 · Sound ★

Zero of 226 issues mention audio. Seed-deterministic ambience — Web Audio, procedural, no samples — driven by the terrain under the viewport: gulls and rope-creak at harbors, wind rising in the ranges, river murmur, a far bell from the capital, weather shifting with the chronicle year. Not music; place-sound. The most Myst-shaped gap in the project: Myst without its soundscape is half a game. And it is a layer, not a surface — it improves the Voyage, the Chronicle, and the Reading Room without adding a page. The only idea here that isn't epic-sized.

Open3 · The Linked Archipelago

No world acknowledges another's existence. A clickable departing ship sails off the neat line to a deterministically neighboring seed, whose gazetteer mentions the world you came from. The linking-book ritual plus Minecraft's infinity: one endless ocean of charted worlds. The voyage engine already knows ocean routes, so the ship is mostly built; the gallery's outward walk becomes diegetic.

Open4 · The Night Sky

No constellation in the backlog. Each culture's syllable grammar names its own invented constellations, drawn as a celestial plate — the Verso's natural sibling: turn the sheet, or look up. It could feed the Daily Hunt a clue written in stars.

Open5 · Systematic Language Drift

The ambitious version of #49 (deep history renamings) and #124 (the Philologist's Glass): sound-change rules applied across the chronicle, so a name shows its etymology through time, and scrubbing the chronicle re-letters the chart — Maraveli → Mravel, "formerly Maraveli."

Risk flag: editing cultures re-rolls every saved seed's names (documented in the README). Drift rules must layer on top of the base draw, never inside it.

Recommendation

With the Reading Room, the Surveyor's Glass, and the Scriptorium all open, the backlog is three epics deep — and everything above except sound is epic-sized. Sound slots in without disturbing the sequencing rulebook. Write the Age is the flagship candidate for the epic after the Scriptorium lands.

Here ends the survey. The unsurveyed remains, as ever, the larger part.
