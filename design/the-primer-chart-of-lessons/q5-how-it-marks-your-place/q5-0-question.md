# Question 5: how does the chart show where you are?

The Primer already says "where you are" in two places. The chart of the atelier, opened from the head, puts its "you are here" on the Primer's own isle (`../x-context/stills/x-today-head-chart-open-1280.png`). The index slip inks "the room you are reading". On this chart "where you are" is the room open on the page, which on arrival is the room the page chose for you (a returning reader's first room with an unsealed step), and moves with the room you open.

## A (Recommended): the open room lit, and the pointing hand at your next step

The open room stands in a gold light, as the slip inks it, and the house's manicule, in the pointing hand's gold ring, points at the next step you can take there.

- What it gives: one "you are here" on the page, not two, and the chart echoes the pointing hand you liked in the rooms.
- What you give up: the words "you are here" from the issue.
- What it costs Sub 11: the gold light and the hand's ring and manicule, drawn in the chart's own sheet in colours the site already names. The hand's glyph is set at about 19.5 px at 1024 and 24.9 px at 1280 (its size on the chart times the chart's scale, computed, not measured).

## B: "you are here" under the open room

The chart of the atelier's own pin: a short tick and the words "you are here".

- What it gives: the issue's words, in the house's existing pin.
- What you give up: two "you are here" on one page, the Primer's isle in the head and the open room on this chart. As drawn, the words also lie across the Reading Room's name (803 square px of overlap at 1280 and 457 at 1024, `../measure/stills.md`); every side of the Explorer has a neighbour within a name's length, so moving them would need a rule of its own.
- What it costs Sub 11: the pin and a place for its words that clears the names.
