## The design

The subsection is an index, not a second home. Its opening paragraph states the test an entry passes and how a later difference joins. A second paragraph says what each of the two environments is and where a CI job's own log says what it ran on, without copying a version or a hardware figure. Then one bullet per case, with a bold lead naming the difference and a "Home:" naming the spec bullet, gate item, errata row, scars entry, flake-record rows or code comment that holds it, by its opening words or as `` `symbol` in `path` ``. The home carries the reading that showed the difference, and the entry does not copy it (ruling 3). A closing paragraph holds the two kinds of case that are not a disagreement: a platform difference both sides hide, and behaviours recorded on one side only.

**The test an entry passes** (written into the opening paragraph so every later joiner applies the same one): a record in the repo says the same thing comes out differently on a Mac and on the CI runner, which means the record says what each side does. A behaviour recorded on one side only, with nothing said of the other, goes to the closing paragraph and never into the list.

The heading is `### Where the Mac and the CI runner disagree` (ruling 2). Issue #1047's builder and Issue #1049 cite the subsection by this heading.

`CLAUDE.md`'s spec table is how a session finds a spec, so its row for `handbook/specs/settle-doctrine.md` names the list in its "what it holds" cell (ruling 1). The "read it before" cell does not change: the moment a reader is sent to the list, a check green on a Mac and red on CI, is Issue #1049's to add.
