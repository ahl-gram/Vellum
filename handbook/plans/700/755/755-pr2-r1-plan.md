## The design

### The contract

1. **A view is one of two words, `"rise"` and `"above"`**: `export const PLATE_VIEWS = ["rise", "above"] as const;` and `export type PlateView = (typeof PLATE_VIEWS)[number];` in `src/site/shared/address-grammar.ts`, beside the grammar's other allowlists. The type is required wherever a plate's identity travels (`ProspectJob`, `ProspectSpec`, `ProspectPlateResult`, `ProspectItem`, `prospectItemFrom`'s argument), so `npm run check` names every caller.
2. **The address states the view from above and nothing else** (D2 (a)): the Prospect room's address carries `view=above` while the bird's-eye is drawn and no `view` key while the rise is, so every address written today, and every door that builds one (the place card, the Reading Room, the Ribbon, the Primer), still opens the rise. `parseProspectAddress` reads `view` through `oneOf(p.get("view"), PLATE_VIEWS)`, `null` when absent or unreadable, and the room reads `addr.view ?? "rise"`.
3. **A gathered sheet carries its view the same way**: a prospect item's emitted form gains `.view-above` after `year` for the bird's-eye and nothing for the rise, so every item string written today emits byte for byte as it does now; `readItem` takes an absent field as `"rise"`, `above` as `"above"`, and drops the item on any other value, `view-rise` included, so each sheet has one spelling (`sameSheet` is byte equality on the emitted item).
4. **The view is part of a sheet's identity**: the view from the rise and the view from above of one place in one year are two sheets, as two years are (TP3 in `test/site/table-address-prospect.test.ts`), and the same view filed twice is refused (call 3).
5. **One glue, both transports.** `prospectResultFor` in `src/site/explorer/prospect-job.ts` draws `engravedBirdseyePlate` for `"above"` and `engravedProspectPlate` for `"rise"`, with the same world, index, dress and year, and returns the view it drew; the worker and the inline copy both call it, so the bird's-eye adds no job kind.
6. **The roads out shed the view** (call 4): `chartTarget` drops `/^(i|year|view)(=|$)/` and `ribbonTarget` drops `/^(i|year|a|b|view)(=|$)/`, as they shed the page's own `i` and `year` (PB3d's rule).
7. **The doors D1 (b) leaves on the rise pass `"rise"`**: the place card's filing (`filingAt` in `src/site/explorer/chart-drawer.ts`) and the Reading Room's plate job (`bindPlates` in `src/site/reading-room/app.ts`).
8. **The names.** The plate names itself "The bird's-eye of <name>, chart <seed>" (Issue #755 call 14, which Alex let stand), so every surface naming the plate follows it: the folio title "The Bird's-eye of <name> · Chart № <seed>", the image's name "The bird's-eye of <name>, chart <seed>", a gathered sheet's title "The Bird's-eye of <name>" and its line "a bird's-eye, <dress>, <year>", the atlas plate "The Bird's-eye of <capital>" (call 5). The folio's second line for a place with no former name reads "<world title> · drawn from above, as a bird sees the town and its country" (the words the spike's stills show; D1 rules them with the switch).

### The bound atlas (call 6)

`prospectPlates` in `src/atlas/compose.ts` returns two plates, the rise view keyed `prospect-capital` as today and then `{ key: "birdseye-capital", title: \`The Bird's-eye of ${name}\`, svg: birdseyePlate(world, capital, STYLES[plateDressFor(bannerStyle)], world.title.year, width) }`. Every consumer already takes the array whole: the served and downloaded document's section "The Prospect of the Capital" (`plateSections` in `src/atlas/document.ts`), the CLI's files (`buildAtlas` in `src/cli/atlas.ts`, which writes `birdseye-capital.svg` by `atlasPlateFilename`), the worker's serializer, and the Print Room's minting under the section `"prospect"` (`renderBoundAtlas` in `src/site/print-room/bound-atlas.ts`). The contents row viii names both, "The prospect of *Laukuwelua*, the bird's-eye of *Laukuwelua*", with two thumbnails, and the folio's plate line for the second reads "plate viii of the bound atlas · the bird's-eye of Laukuwelua".

### Alternatives rejected

- **A job kind of its own for the bird's-eye.** `prospectResultFor` is the one glue both transports call (`handbook/specs/site-architecture.md`), and a second kind doubles the worker's switch, the inline copy and every door's job for a plate that differs in one argument.
- **A dynamic import of the bird's-eye inside the job,** so a page drawing only the rise never loads it. It makes `prospectResultFor` asynchronous for one branch and saves 19.4 KB gzip on a page that downloads a 200 KB plate; the plate's code already loads only when a plate is drawn (Issue #801).
- **An `AtlasComposition` field of its own (`birdseyes`).** It adds a type field, a serializer line, a section, a contents row and a mint for one plate that belongs to the prospect's subject; the array already carries several plates of one subject, as the regions and the draughtings do.
- **A contents row of its own for the bird's-eye.** No numeral is free (recon), and adding xiii renumbers four rows that Issue #465 ruling 7 numbered.
- **The view in the place card, the Reading Room or the Ribbon's road's end.** D1 (b) keeps them on the rise.
- **The view written into the Explorer's own hash.** The Explorer draws no plate; its table key carries each sheet's view.
