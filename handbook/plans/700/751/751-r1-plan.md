## The design

**One toggle press, `#zoom-hold`, in the Glass corner on the Explorer only**, named "Hold the sheet", an anchor glyph, lit while held by the Glass's lit dress, and while it is lit the caption line under the chart's title ends with the mark `held`.

**What the hold does:**

1. While held, a camera settle still writes the address but never reaches the redraft: nothing finer is drafted, no sibling window is drafted on a pan, and a zoom out takes nothing away. The held sheet, a region inset or the bare world sheet, stays on the table whatever the camera does.
2. While held, the camera's ceiling rises from 8 to 24, so a held sheet can be read at up to three times the size today's deepest view gives (the spike: a hamlet label is 8.09 px at k=8 today and 24.26 px held at k=24, against an 8 px village label at rest).
3. Pressing hold drops a draft still in flight: a survey that has not landed is not on the table.
4. Pressing again lets go: the ceiling returns to 8, and today's settle runs at once for the camera where it stands, so a band-1 sheet held at k=8 is replaced by the band-3 draft on the spot. Let go while the camera, or the glide in flight, is headed for a depth above 8, and the camera glides to 8 instead, the settle at its landing doing the same. Let go while a settle is still to come, and nothing runs at the press: that settle runs when it was going to, with the hold already off (call 14). A settle is still to come while a glide is in flight (here, one toward a depth at or under 8: it lands where it was headed and its landing settles, as every glide's does) and while the camera moved within the last settle debounce (a glide just landed, a wheel or a drag just ended, or one still running), which is what the controller's `settlePending()` answers. The settle at once is for a camera at rest.
   **Why no settle at once while a settle is still to come:** two settles would run. If the camera has stopped, both judge the same camera and send the same survey: the second bumps `regionGen`, the first job's result is thrown away, and the one first-in-first-out worker still runs it, so the inset lands a job late (the plan skeptic measured it on "Stand off" followed by the hold 350 to 450 ms later). If the camera is still moving, the first judges a camera about to leave. The landing's settle cannot always undo what it sent: `decideSettle` (`src/world/lod.ts`) judges against the committed sheet, since `dispatchRegion` leaves `currentBand` and `currentWindow` (`src/site/explorer/lod-controller.ts`) as they were until `commitInset`, so a landing that finds nothing to change sends nothing, bumps no `regionGen`, and the early draft commits. Measured with `decideSettle` and `bandFor` (UP = 1.584, 3.168, 6.336; DOWN = 1.263, 2.525, 5.051): a band-1 sheet held at k=2, zoomed to 4, "Stand off" pressed twice and let go mid-glide; a settle at once at 4 sends band 2, the landing at 2.04 is a noop against band 1, and band 2 would stand at 2.04, where today's settle keeps band 1.
   **Why the pending target can be trusted:** the controller clears it whenever a glide stops: when it ends, when it is interrupted mid-flight, and, from revision 1, when it is cancelled before its first frame. d3 fires `cancel`, not `interrupt`, on a transition stopped before it starts (`node_modules/d3-transition/src/interrupt.js`), which `zoomTo`, `reset`, `rebase`, `refit`, a wheel, a mouse press or a touch on the sheet does to a glide queued in the same frame (a second press of "Draw nearer" or "Stand off" does not: its glide waits for the earlier one to start and then interrupts it), and `glideTo` listened for `end interrupt` alone, so the target stood after such a stop (measured: a queued "Draw nearer" then `zoomTo` in one task leaves the pending target at 1.4 on the built controller, and null with `cancel` added). Left standing, a let-go would read a glide that never lands: settle never at a camera at rest, or glide the camera in to 8 from under it.
   **The double-click glide, which the Glass does not start:** d3-zoom's own double-click (and a double tap, which its touch handler routes to the same function) runs a glide of its own, never through `glideTo`, and fixes that glide's end at the double-click, clamped against the ceiling then in force (`dblclicked` in `node_modules/d3-zoom/src/zoom.js`: `t1` is computed at the event and passed to `schedule` as a fixed transform). Held at 6, a double-click heads for 12; a let-go before the camera passes 8 would see no pending target and a camera under 8, and the glide would land at 12 with the ceiling at 8 (the plan skeptic measured it, and the planner reproduced it: lands at 12 for a let-go 0 to 120 ms in, at 9 from 4.5). So lowering the ceiling stops, where it stands, any glide on the viewport not started through `glideBy` (`setScaleMax`, below), and the let-go then reads the camera where the glide stopped: under 8, nothing more glides and the settle the glide's frames armed runs once; above 8, the camera glides back to 8 (measured with the fix: the camera rests at 6.005, 6.158 and 7.536 for let-gos 20, 60 and 120 ms in, each with one settle there; 200 ms in, at 11.7, it glides back to 8; in the same task, it rests at 6 with one settle at once).
   What the stop does not know is where the double-click was headed, so it cuts short every double-click glide met by a let-go, for the whole of its 250 ms, wherever it was going: one headed under 8 too (held at 3, a double-click toward 6 rests at 3.09, 4.86 or 5.96 for a let-go 60, 150 or 220 ms in), and a shift-double-click zoom-out from above 8 (from 12 toward 6, stopped near 11, then glided to 8 rather than landing on 6). That is the one difference from a press's glide, which a let-go leaves to land where it was headed under 8 (call 14 and ZH8): the controller knows a press's target and not a double-click's. When the stop matters to ruling 1 depends on where the double-click began: a held camera at 4.2, 6 or 7.5 passes 8 at about 197, 126 or 73 ms into the glide (the skeptic's measurement), and only a let-go before that would otherwise land the camera above 8. **One frame it misses:** the stop trusts a pending target to mean a glide of the controller's own is in flight, and d3's double-click does not interrupt a press's glide already flying until its own glide starts on the next frame, so a let-go in that one frame skips the stop, reads the press's target, leaves the coming settle to run, and the double-click lands above 8 (the plan skeptic's fifth round measured 10.09 for a let-go in the same task as the double-click, a press's glide toward 7 in flight; 30 ms later it rests at 5.12). It takes a press, a double-click and the hold inside about 16 ms. Whether the stop is the right answer at all is open decision D1, below; the plan is written to its recommended option (a). Any other option changes `zoomCeiling` in all three controller diffs, test 7, rows `u10a` to `u10c`, doctrine edits 2 and 6, and this paragraph, which the stop condition on D1 makes a revision.
   The Glass's home glide (`glideHome`) is the only other glide not started through `glideBy`. No home runs while the sheet is held, since every home lets go before it moves the camera, so a lowered ceiling meets one only if the reader presses hold during a home's 300 ms and lets go inside them; the stop then leaves the camera where the home glide stood, skips `glideHome`'s landing callback (its address write), and the settle the home's frames armed writes the address and judges that camera instead, after `easeHome` has already taken the inset away (the plan skeptic's fifth round measured it: from 6, held at 60 ms and let go at 100 ms, the home rests at 4.94 with one settle there and no landing callback). One mouse reaches it: the whole-sheet press, then the hold twice inside 300 ms. It is part of D1's option (a).
   **Why the glide's pending target is what the let-go reads, and not the camera:** `glideBy` compounds against the pending target (`nextGlideTarget` in `src/site/shared/zoom-controller.ts`), so the factor has to be taken against that target. Read the camera instead and a let-go during a "Stand off" glide from above 8 re-aims the glide at pending times 8 over the camera, further out than 8: from k=24, one press of "Stand off" and the let-go in the same task land at 5.71, not 8, and a let-go a few frames into the same glide lands near 7.08 (measured 2026-10-20 in a Node probe that drives the branch's own controller at `15f569a0` with the real d3, `751-r1-probe-letgo.ts` beside this plan; it runs from a folder under a tree's `out/` holding a copy of that controller).
   **What d3-zoom does to a glide when the ceiling drops under it**, read in `node_modules/d3-zoom/src/zoom.js` (`schedule`, whose tween computes the end transform when it is initialised) and `node_modules/d3-transition/src/transition/schedule.js` (`start`, which initialises the tween at the transition's first timer callback): a glide's end is fixed at that first callback and clamped to the ceiling in force then, and its frames are never clamped afterwards. So a glide queued toward a depth above 8 in the same task as the let-go lands on 8 by itself (measured: from k=8, "Draw nearer" and the let-go in one task land on 8 with or without the pending read, which is why the old ZH7 could not bite), while one already under way keeps its end (measured: let go one frame into a glide from 8 toward 11.2 with no glide back issued, and it lands on 11.2).
5. Every path that homes the camera lets go first, with no settle of its own: a draw (its `rebase`), the turned sheet and the survey ink (their `homeToWorld`), and the voiced home (the whole-sheet press and the 0 key, `goHomeVoiced`). Each lowers the ceiling back to 8 as it lets go.
6. The address never carries the hold, and carries the camera no deeper than 8: a link copied while held opens unheld at the same centre, at k=8 at most, so `readHash`'s ratified `k <= 8` bound stands.

**Where it lives:** the hold's state is pure in a new module and composed in `createGlass` (`src/site/explorer/glass.ts`), beside the redraft it gates and every home it releases on; `regionEligible` in `src/site/explorer/app.ts` keeps the dress, survey and flip gates as documented. The press is seated through a new named slot, `presses`, in `src/layouts/Glass.astro`, under the touch press and above "Draw nearer", so the kit component keeps one shape on the nine pages that seat it and the Explorer owns its own press. The Glass's lit dress, written today for the touch press alone, is lifted to every toggle press in the corner.

**Rejected, with the reason:**

- *Reuse the test-only `redraftEnabled` flag as the hold.* It is the e2e suites' switch (eleven call sites set it through `__vellumSetRedraftEnabled`), so a press bound to it would light and unlight under the suites, and it carries no let-go settle, no home release and no ceiling.
- *A press inside the region inset, like the dog-ear.* It cannot serve the second moment, before any region sheet exists, and the issue rules one control with two moments.
- *A prop on `Glass.astro` that renders an Explorer-only press inside the kit.* The kit would then carry one page's press; `site-architecture.md` keeps a component one shape with the page deciding what stands in it, and a named slot is how `LegendRow.astro` already takes a page's content.
- *A refusing dress on dresses where the redraft never runs (topographic, ink, nautical).* Holding a sheet that would not redraft anyway is still a hold, so the press never refuses; the spike's refusing dress read almost the same as the lit one.
- *Raising the ceiling for every reader, held or not.* That moves the Glass's own schedule, which the rulebook gives to Issue #395's last sub; the held ceiling magnifies a sheet already drawn and adds no band.
- *Writing the true k above 8 into the address.* `readHash` refuses a camera above 8 whole, so the link would open at the whole sheet, the furthest picture from the one sent.
- *(Revision 1) A let-go that drops the pending read and always re-aims through `glideBy(1)`.* It would clamp a glide in flight or the camera under the new ceiling in one line, but every let-go at rest at or under 8 would then settle one glide plus one settle debounce later instead of at once (call 2), through a glide that goes nowhere; the explicit branch stays.
- *(Revision 1) A let-go that settles at once whatever is in flight*, as first built. Design item 4 says why: mid-glide, the early settle's draft can outlive a landing that sends nothing, and stand a band today's settle never leaves.
- *(Revision 1) A let-go that settles at once whenever no glide is queued*, the first fold of the skeptic's first finding. A camera that has just stopped, or is still under a wheel or a finger, has its own settle armed, and the let-go would run a second one beside it: the same survey sent twice, or a camera about to leave judged (the skeptic's second round, measured on the controller). `settlePending()` asks about both.
- *(Revision 1) Routing d3-zoom's double-click through `glideTo`, so the controller tracks its target and the let-go re-aims it.* It replaces d3's double-click and double-tap handling on every page that seats the Glass (its anchor point, its 250 ms duration, its reduced-motion path). Open decision D1's option (b) gets the same landing by noting the double-click's target beside d3's own handling instead, so if Alex wants the landing, (b) is how it is built, not this.
- *(Revision 1) Leaving the pending target uncleared on `cancel`.* The let-go then has no truthful way to know a glide is in flight; the one-word listener change is the fix at the source, and it changes nothing for a glide that starts (measured: two queued presses from k=2 still land on 3.92 with it).

## Open decisions

**D1. What letting go of the hold does in the quarter second after a double-click on the map.** A double-click (or a double tap) on the map zooms in two times, gliding there over a quarter of a second, and while the sheet is held that glide may head deeper than the usual limit of 8 times. If the reader lets go of the hold during that quarter second, something has to decide where the zoom ends. Reaching it takes a deliberate two-handed act on a tablet, and is close to impossible with one mouse.

- **(a) Recommended: the zoom stops where it is at the moment of letting go**, and if that is already deeper than 8 times it glides back to 8, as letting go always does. Two lines in the shared zoom code, which only the hold calls. The catches: a double-click that would have ended under 8 also stops short (held at 3 times, a double-click toward 6 stops wherever it had got to, for instance at 4.9 times if let go 0.15 seconds in), and so does a zoom-out double-click; the same stop catches the whole-sheet button's glide home if the hold is pressed twice during it, which one mouse can do, and leaves the map partway home; and in the one sixtieth of a second after a double-click made while a "Draw nearer" or "Stand off" glide is still moving, the stop misses and the map lands deeper than 8 until the next touch, which no hand can reach. The plan is written to this option.
- **(b) The zoom finishes where the double-click was heading, but never deeper than 8.** The shared zoom code would note where each double-click on any page is heading, so letting go re-aims it the way it re-aims a press of "Draw nearer" or "Stand off"; the glide home is left alone and lands at home. More code in the part every chart room shares, not yet measured; choosing it means a further revision of this plan.
- **(c) The zoom finishes where the double-click was heading, and if that is deeper than 8, the map eases back to 8 on its own about half a second later.** The Explorer's own "the map has come to rest" step pulls an unheld map back under 8 whatever put it there, so it also closes the one-sixtieth-of-a-second gap and leaves the glide home alone, and the shared zoom code needs no stop at all. The cost is the visible overshoot in that moment. Measured in the planner's test harness (every case ends at or under 8, one survey drafted, the home lands at home), not yet in a browser; choosing it means a further revision of this plan.
- **(d) As first built: the zoom finishes where the double-click was heading even when that is deeper than 8**, and stays there until the next touch, or the next press of "Draw nearer", pulls it back (that press zooms out, to 8). A copied link already says 8. Against ruling 1's "letting go glides back to 8", so not recommended.
