## The design

**One home for a mark's size: `src/render/layers/settlement-marks.ts`.** Every mark is drawn at `k * scale`, where `k` is the sheet's own `widthPx / 1500` and `scale` is:

- on a world sheet (`world.region === undefined`): 1, so every world sheet draws byte for byte what it draws today;
- on a close-up: the tier's step on the ladder (`REGION_MARK_LADDER`), times the growth for the close-up's detail level (`markGrowth`);
- in the key (`legend-icons.ts`): the ladder alone, never the growth (ruling 4).

**The ladder** is ladder B exactly as the designer drew it (`design/settlement-mark-sizes/mock/ladder.ts`, `LADDERS[2]`): capital 1.5, seat 1.5, town 1, village 1, hamlet 1, farmstead `3.6 / (4.8 * HOUSE_SCALE)` (0.4167), mill `3.6 / (2 * WHEEL_RADIUS)` (0.4615). The 3.6 is the farmstead's and the mill's larger side at a 1500 px sheet, `FINER_MARK_SPAN`. Strokes scale with the mark, as the designer's mock drew them (its `k` carried the multiplier into every stroke width).

**The growth** is `2 ** (min(detail, MARK_GROWTH_DEEPEST) / MARK_GROWTH_DOUBLING)` with `MARK_GROWTH_DOUBLING = 3` and `MARK_GROWTH_DEEPEST = 5`: a third of a doubling per level, about 1.26 a band, 3.17 at band 5, exactly the designer's law (`growAt` in `design/settlement-mark-sizes/mock/ladder.ts`, `2 ** (1/3)` per band). It stops at level 5, the Glass's deepest band (`handbook/specs/rulebook.md`: the Glass descends to band 5 and no further; the hold magnifies band 5 and adds no band). `detail` is the close-up's survey detail level, which `regionDetailLevel` in `src/world/region.ts` already computes from the spec: the band on every region the Glass draws (they are all `detail: true`), 0 on a bare survey.

**The detail level is carried on the region world.** `generateRegionWorld` stamps `detail: regionDetailLevel(spec)` into `world.region` (through `regionCarry`), so the renderer reads it where it draws the marks and no render option, worker message or `RenderCtx` member changes. Consequences, each the one wanted:
- The Glass's insets, the Chart Table drawer and the Portfolio (which redraw a gathered survey through the same worker `region` job, `src/site/explorer/chart-drawer.ts`) all draw the grown marks; no file under `src/site/` changes.
- The bound atlas's regional plates (`regionPlates` in `src/atlas/compose.ts`, a bare survey of a 0.38 window, `detail` unset) carry level 0 and draw the ladder alone, which is exactly what the atlas still showed (`design/settlement-mark-sizes/stills/sheet-atlas.png`, drawn with `hold`, no growth).
- A test or script that generates a detailed band-5 region and renders it without a `regionRecipe` draws the same grown marks the Explorer does.

**The name's gap.** `nameSetting` in `src/render/layers/settlements.ts` adds to its tier's gap however far the mark's half-width grew past today's, never less than nothing: `max(0, scale - 1) * MARK_HALF_WIDTH[tier]` at `k`, where `MARK_HALF_WIDTH` holds the half-widths of today's marks in the antique dress (capital `5.5 * CAPITAL_GLYPH_SCALE`, seat `5.5 * SEAT_GLYPH_SCALE`, town 3.4, village 2.3, hamlet 1.5, farmstead `2.4 * HOUSE_SCALE`, mill `WHEEL_RADIUS`). That is the designer's gap rule (`gapShift` in `design/settlement-mark-sizes/mock/ladder.ts`), so a name to the east or west of its mark stands exactly where the stills put it. A ruined settlement keeps today's gap. The gap is computed inside `nameSetting`, so its signature and its one test caller (`test/render/close-up-names.test.ts`) do not change.

**A name is never set on its own mark, on a close-up.** The designer's gap is a half-width, but a castle stands on its point and rises 8 units for every 5.5 it spreads to a side, so the gap that clears its sides does not clear its top. Measured at a 1500 px sheet in antique (`out/1104-dry-run/gap-geometry.txt`, `node out/1104-dry-run/gap-geometry.ts`), the capital's north spot lies on its own castle from band 1 down, by 0.5 px at band 1 and 9.5 px at band 5, and its north-east and north-west spots by 0.9 px at band 5; a seat's north spot from band 3 down, by 0.6 to 4.4 px. Every other tier, and the east and west spots of every tier, clear their marks at every depth. So `placeSettlementLabel` refuses, on a close-up, any spot whose box meets the box the settlement's own mark is drawn in, before it tries the arena. The east spot, the first tried and the one a capital, seat or town is forced into, always clears (east 7.0, 6.8 and 6.0 px), so the refusal never costs a name the forcing would have kept, and a name that took the east spot in the stills takes it still. On a world sheet nothing is refused, so no world-sheet name moves. (A world sheet already has one such spot today, a topographic or nautical capital's south spot over its star by 1.9 px; it joins the errata below rather than move the committed charts.)

**The footprint.** A farmstead or a mill is placed only where the land under its whole mark is land (`snapToStandingLand` with `MARK_FOOTPRINT` in `src/society/farmsteads.ts`). Grown at band 5 the mill's wheel is 11.43 px across at a 1500 px sheet (`7.8 * 0.4615 * 3.1748`), half-span 5.71, and one cell of a 320-wide close-up is 4.276 px (`(1500 - 2 * marginFor(1500)) / 319`), so the wheel reaches 1.34 cells below its point, past today's `down: 1`. `down` becomes 2, the next whole cell. The house grown is 11.43 tall and 5.71 half-wide: 2.67 cells up and 1.34 aside, inside today's `up: 3` and `side: 2`, which stay. That is geometry, not a fitted number. One block serves both shapes because the snap runs before the mark is known to be a mill (`placeFarmsteads` decides the kind from the snapped cell), so a farmstead takes the deeper block too. Measured over the 307 settlement-centred band-5 windows of seeds 1 to 12 (2,346 marks): 43 move, 40 by one cell and 3 by one diagonal cell (at most 6 px at a 1500 px sheet, about 2.8 px on screen at 1280 by 800), none is lost, none changes between farmstead and mill.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Growth from the window alone** (`detailForWindow(world.region.window)`, in the renderer). Simpler by one field, but the atlas's 0.38 plate rounds to level 1 and its marks would grow 1.26 times past what its still showed, a change to a ruled picture (`handbook/specs/conventions.md`, the fidelity rule's fourth consequence). Carrying the survey's own detail level keeps the atlas as drawn.
- **Growth through a render option** (`opts.regionRecipe.detail`, passed from `labelledLayers` in `src/render/map-renderer.ts` into `settlementsLayer`). Draws the same bytes as the chosen design on every sheet measured (the two variants' digests agree on all 15 region sheets of `out/1104-dry-run/control.ts`), but every test that renders a detailed band-5 close-up without a `regionRecipe` would draw ungrown marks the Explorer never shows, and the worker would become the only source of a mark's size.
- **Changing `HOUSE_SCALE` and `WHEEL_RADIUS`** (the issue body's mutation). It would shrink the shapes but leave their strokes at today's widths, heavier than the stills drew them, and the growth would still need its own multiplier. The ladder carries the shrink and the strokes with it, in one place with the growth.
- **The reach gap** (the gap grows by how far the mark rises above its point, the castle's height, instead of its half-width). It keeps every candidate off the castle without any refusal, but sets a capital's eastern name 18.8 px from its castle at band 5 where the stills set it 7.0 (`out/1104-dry-run/gap-geometry.txt`), a visible departure from what Alex saw.
- **The designer's stills' exact multiplier per band read from `LOD_BANDS`.** The house rule keys depth to the window, never to a band index (`handbook/specs/region-and-voyage.md`); the detail level is the window's.
- **Pinning exact pixel sizes in tests.** Every value here is a feel value, provisional until the post-use review; `handbook/specs/conventions.md` lets dressing-level pins lag one live-use cycle. The tests pin the relations the rulings state (the order, the growth's threading and its monotone rise, the key at the ladder, the world sheet untouched) and the measurements go in the evidence.
