## The design

Revision 1's design stands (its lines 66 to 120) except where this section says otherwise.

### When a suite's first step starts its clock (call C21)

The lane log prints no line when a suite starts (`grep -n "console.log" e2e/run.ts e2e/support/suites.ts e2e/support/step.ts e2e/support/runner.ts` finds the fingerprint, the skip line, the never-reached line and the runner's own output only). The result line before a suite's first step belongs to the previous suite, and the reading from it carries that suite's closing steps, which print nothing on a pass: slide-away's first step read from region-detail's RD5 line carries region-detail's `RD restore` step, 1.4 to 2.1 s (Measurements). So a suite's first step is timed from its suite's start, computed from two lines every lane log prints: the time on its `fingerprint:` line, after which `runSelected` (`e2e/support/suites.ts`) runs the suites back to back, plus the `per-suite wall clock` the lane prints for each suite its `tier:` line names before this one, in that order (the wall-clock block is sorted by time, the `tier:` line by run order). Measured on 12 lane logs, the first suite's start computed back from the end of the lane falls within 0.16 s of the fingerprint line, and where the previous suite ends on a printing check (motion after render, turn after motion) the computed start falls within 0.04 s of that line. Each wall clock is printed to a tenth of a second, so a deep suite's start can drift by a few tenths, which is nothing against a 10-second line.

Alex's R1 has the pull request that adds a suite read that step from its own run like any other new check, so the reading counts what the suite runs before its first step, its own set-up, which the pull request wrote. That is the "Who finds" bullet's case, and it takes no exemption.

### Set-up a pull would not save, for a step a list finds (call C21)

A reading also carries what the suite runs between the earlier result line and the step's start without printing and without the unit rule counting it with the step. The spec names it set-up when it is a step with no check of its own, or code outside every step that navigates, waits or sleeps; code that does none of those (a kit built, a CDP send that sets emulation) is not set-up, so a reader can tell from the suite's own code whether a step owes what follows, and a line of plain code does not trigger it. (A step with no check whose state the step reads is counted with it by the unit rule in the "Too slow" bullet, so its seconds are the unit's own.) Pulling the step does not save that set-up. None of the nine units owes it today: each is preceded by its suite's own printing check with nothing between but H13d, before which `e2e/suites/home.ts` sends `Emulation.setEmulatedMedia` and nothing else (Measurements). Revision 1 said such a step "is not called too slow on that reading alone: the plan that would pull or rework it first reads its own seconds", with no method; no line marks a step's start in a normal run. A run in which the step is skipped does: `makeStep` (`e2e/support/step.ts`) prints `<step>, Issue #<n>: skipped on purpose, not run` at the moment the step would have begun (in run 40198357203, EA7's skip line is 0.4 ms after EA1's result and FL1's 0.3 ms after CO7's, where nothing runs between).

So the set-up is read first, before anything is spent on the step: the pull request of its one honest fix first pushes the unit's `E2E_SKIPS` entry, naming every step of the unit so that set-up the unit owns is skipped with it (round r2, finding 6), and reads the set-up in that run and two more runs of the lane's job started by hand, as the time from the earlier result line to the skip line of the unit's first step, the median of three like every reading the rule takes (finding 5's single-run noise). The step is judged on main's reading less that set-up: under 10 seconds, it is not too slow, and that pull request closes unmerged with its issue, both readings recorded; otherwise its next commit takes the entry out again with the fix, and the after reading is taken less the same set-up. The entry is lawful on that branch, since its issue is open (`scripts/e2e-skips-open.ts`), and naming every step of the unit is what item 11 asks of any pull. A pull request that adds or lengthens a check takes no such exemption: R1 applies "the same 10-second test" to what its own run reads, and the set-up in that reading is what it chose to put before its step.

### When one reading is near the line (call C22)

Ruling D5 B: "One log read, and two re-runs only when the check is near the line". Near is derived from the run-to-run spread, by `CLAUDE.md`'s rule for a bound: sweep first, set it past the worst case, and name the headroom. The principle: a single reading is near the line when the widest spread a single run has been measured to show could put the step's median on the other side of 10 seconds.

Measured on unchanged code only, so a code change does not read as runner spread: three groups of consecutive main runs between which nothing a lane runs changed (`src`, `e2e`, `scripts`, `package.json`, `package-lock.json`, `design/kit`, `astro.config.ts`, `vite.config.ts` and `.github`), 4, 3 and 3 runs. Over every span of 5 seconds or more present in every run of its group, 258 spans and 859 readings, a single reading ran from **0.440** to **1.882** of its group's median (Z21c to ZH1 read 2.4 s against 5.4 and 5.5; CO2 to CO3 read 29.3 s against 15.6 and 15.1). A step whose median is 10 seconds can therefore read anywhere from 4.40 to 18.82 seconds on one run, and a reading anywhere in that range can belong to a step on either side of the line. The band is set outward to **4 to 20 seconds**, 0.40 s of headroom below and 1.18 s above (ratios 0.40 and 2.00). Two checks it holds against: five more same-code pairs, 424 spans, stay inside it (worst pair 5.5 s against 9.8 s); and the 25 runs revision 1 read, whose code moved, give 0.509 to 1.783 over the 66 spans present in all 25. Spans under 5 seconds are left out: their ratio is dominated by the log's line timing, and no step whose median is near 10 seconds is among them.

**Its price.** In the four newest main runs, of 513 to 523 batches of result lines per run (the nearest the log comes to steps), 401 to 413 read under 4 s, 90 to 106 from 4 to 20 s, and 14 to 16 over 20 s, so about 1 in 5.5 readings falls in the band; the narrower band from the 1st and 99th percentiles (0.607 and 1.288, so 6.1 to 12.9 s) would catch 41 to 50, about 1 in 11. The two runs are owed only for a step its plan counts thin, so a pull request whose new check is thin and reads 4 to 20 s pays two hand-started runs of one lane's job, each a lane job's length (medians 445 to 583 s over 25 runs, revision 1's measurements), started one after the other: about 15 to 20 minutes of waiting. The percentile band halves how often that is paid and is rejected because it is not past the worst case: a reading at 13 to 18 s outside it can belong to a step whose median is under 10.

So, for a step its plan counts thin (at most two claims held nowhere else, on at most two pages or rooms): one reading under 4 seconds passes, one over 20 is too slow, and one from 4 to 20 takes the two runs and the median. A step that is not thin has no line to be near, so its one reading is recorded and nothing more is owed (round r2, finding 8). The band is stated once, in the "Who finds" bullet, with its date and this plan as its provenance, since runner hardware changes and the band is re-derived by the same sweep when it does; the lists and the honest fix read three runs throughout, as ruling D2 and the cost bullet say. Z25 shows why one run alone cannot decide inside the band: on unchanged code it read 29.0, 34.9, 36.6 and 23.0 s in the four newest runs.
