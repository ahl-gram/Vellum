## The design

The design below is the one Alex ruled on 2026-11-03 (comment 6613336379): each waiting strip is hidden whole (Q1), blank paper shows where it waits (Q2), and the strip positions travel with every scroll (Q3).

**Which plates ink in (ruled).** `draw` in `src/site/ribbon/app.ts` takes a third argument, `ceremony: boolean`. The boot draw (the file's last statement) passes `true`; the Turn about press (`swap`'s click) passes `true`; both pickers' `change` handlers pass `false`. "The first plate of a visit" is the boot draw's plate and needs no flag of its own: the selects are filled only by a draw that succeeded (`fillSelect` runs in the resolve) and Turn about is inert while `last` is null, so a failed boot draw leaves nothing that can draw next.

**The reveal's lifecycle, in a new `src/site/ribbon/ink.ts`.** `bindInk({ plate, ground, stage })` returns `{ begin, hold, retire }`, bound once in `app.ts` beside `bindRibbonRoom`, with `furniture.plate`, `furniture.map` (the ground: the camera's transform target, so the ground rides every pan and zoom with the plate) and `furniture.stage`.

- `retire()` takes the reveal off the plate at once: it bumps the binding's generation counter, removes the class `rb-inking` from the plate, removes the plate's inline `clip-path`, `--rb-strips` and `animation-play-state`, and removes the ground's inline `background-color`. It is idempotent.
- `hold()` freezes a reveal where it stands: it bumps the counter (so a run still waiting on its plate's decode never starts) and, when the plate carries `rb-inking`, writes the inline `animation-play-state: paused`. On a plate with no reveal it changes nothing on screen.
- `begin(strips, paper)` runs `retire()`; then, if `matchMedia("(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)").matches` (read at each call, never stored), it returns: under reduced motion the plate is shown whole the moment it decodes and takes no reveal at all. Otherwise it keeps the counter's value as its own, writes on the plate `--rb-strips` (the strip count), `clip-path` (`inkClip(strips)`, below) and `animation-play-state: paused`, writes the ground's `background-color` (the plate's paper), forces a style flush (`void plate.getBoundingClientRect()`), then adds `rb-inking`. Then `plate.decode()`; once it resolves, it waits for every animation then running on the stage (`stage.getAnimations()` whose `playState` is `"running"`, each `finished` with its rejection caught), and then, if the counter is still its own, removes the inline `animation-play-state`, which starts the run. If `decode()` rejects and the counter is still its own, it calls `retire()`, so a plate that cannot decode shows whatever the browser shows of it, unclipped.
- One `animationend` listener on the plate, added by `bindInk`: when the event's `animationName` is `"rb-ink"` and no `rb-ink` animation on the plate is still `"running"`, it calls `retire()`. This is the end-of-animation half of the house's one-shot recipe; the other half is the deterministic retire at the top of every draw (`app.ts`, below), and both are owed because hiding or replacing a plate mid-run fires no event (`handbook/specs/ui-design.md`, "A one-shot ceremony needs its own guard").
- The flush before the class is added is what restarts the animation when a Turn about plate replaces one still revealing: the held plate still carries the class when `begin` runs, so `begin`'s own `retire()` removes it in the same task that adds it again, and without a style recalculation between the two Chrome keeps the old animation at whatever frame it stood. RB22's fourth step pins it (the turned plate must run, and from under half way), so it carries no comment.
- The calls in `app.ts`. At the top of `draw` (after `const t0 = ...`): `if (ceremony) ink.hold(); else ink.retire();`, so a picked journey ends a reveal at the press and Turn about freezes it until the turned plate arrives. In the resolve, right after `showPlate(...)` and before `sheet.rebase()`: `if (ceremony) ink.begin(res.strips, res.paper);`, so the clip and the ground are on the new plate in the same task its `src` is set and it is never painted whole before its reveal, and the old plate, held, is never painted whole between the press and the new plate either. In the catch, after the `myGen !== drawGen` return: `ink.retire();`, so a draw that fails leaves no reveal held on the plate it leaves on screen. In the row's lean callback, before `sheet.lean(row.nx, row.ny)`: `ink.retire();`. Every way out of a draw therefore retires or begins afresh, which is the deterministic half of the house's recipe; a draw superseded by a newer one returns early and the newer draw's own top and resolve do it.

**The clip.** `inkClip(strips: ReadonlyArray<StripBox>): string` in `ink.ts` returns one `polygon(evenodd, ...)`: the whole plate (`0 0, 100% 0, 100% 100%, 0 100%, 0 0`), then for each strip `i` of `n` a hole joined to the corner `0 0` before and after it, so the joins have no area and the even-odd rule cuts each hole out. A strip's hole is the box from `L = x0 - PAD_X` to `R = x0 + w + PAD_X` across and from `T = y0 - PAD_TOP` to its moving foot `E` down, each divided by `RIBBON_W` or `RIBBON_H` and written as a percentage with at most four decimals (`` `${Number((v * 100).toFixed(4))}%` ``); its vertices in order are `L T, R T, R E, L E, L T`; `E` is `` `calc(${B}% - ${B - T}% * clamp(0, var(--rb-ink) * ${n} - ${i}, 1))` `` with `B = y0 + h + PAD_BOTTOM`. So while `--rb-ink` runs from 0 to 1, strip `i` uncovers from its foot to its head over the `i`-th of `n` equal parts of the run, the bottom-left strip first, and at 1 every hole has no height. `PAD_X = 7` (the middle of the 14-unit gap between strips; the strips' ink runs at most 1.59 past their boxes), `PAD_TOP = 4` (the ink at most 1.35 past the head; the title band's last line 8 above), `PAD_BOTTOM = 2` (1.35 past the foot; the colophon's text box 1 below, its capitals' ink lower), all in the plate's own units (finding 2). The title banner, the double frame and the colophon are outside every hole, so they stand from the start.

**The ground.** While a strip waits, what shows through its hole is `#map`, the plate's transform target, painted the plate's own paper by `begin` (`res.paper`: `#f2e8cf` on the antique dress, which is `--chart-paper`, and `#faf7ef` on ink), and the grain of the plate's paper does not show against it (finding 5).

**The run.** In `src/styles/ribbon/index.css`: `@property --rb-ink { syntax: "<number>"; inherits: false; initial-value: 1; }`, `@keyframes rb-ink { from { --rb-ink: 0; } to { --rb-ink: 1; } }`, the token `--rb-ink-strip: 450ms` in the sheet's existing `:root` block beside `--folio-cap`, and `#rb-plate.rb-inking { animation: rb-ink calc(var(--rb-ink-strip) * var(--rb-strips, 3)) linear; }`. No fill mode: at the run's end `--rb-ink` returns to its initial 1 (the whole plate) until the listener retires the clip, so no filled clip outlives the run to cut the plate's hover shadow (`handbook/specs/ui-design.md`, "An entrance animation with a `both` or `forwards` fill"). 450 ms a strip, linear: a 3-strip road reveals in 1.35 s, seed 42's default road (6 strips) in 2.7 s, a 7-strip road in 3.15 s.

**The plate stands still while it inks.** `#rb-plate.rb-inking:hover { transform: none; box-shadow: none; }` (specificity 1,2,0 over `.plate:hover`'s 0,2,0 in `src/styles/shared/motion.css`). The house lift (Issue #514's open question) returns untouched the moment the class retires.

**Reduced motion.** `begin` takes no reveal under reduced motion (above), so the plate is whole on the first frame it is decoded. The blanket alone is not enough: the plate reader found the spike's first build, which left reduced motion to the blanket, painting one frame of bare paper (about 11 ms) before the whole plate, because a plate classed and held for its decode is clipped on its first decoded frame whatever the run's duration (finding 9). A reveal already running when the reader turns reduced motion on is still collapsed by the blanket in `src/styles/shared/motion.css` (0.01 ms, no delay: finding 4) and retires on that `animationend`, since the run is CSS.

**Print.** The Ribbon sheet's `@media print` block gains `background: none !important;` on `#map` and `clip-path: none !important;` on `#rb-plate`, so a page printed mid-reveal prints the whole plate on bare paper: the inline styles lose to an author `!important` rule and to nothing else (`handbook/specs/cascade-traps.md`).

**The wire (Q3, ruled).** `RibbonPlateData` in `src/site/explorer/ribbon-job.ts` gains `readonly strips: ReadonlyArray<StripBox>` (each strip's `x0`, `y0`, `w`, `h` in the plate's own units, from the same `layoutRibbon(input)` the plate is drawn from) and `readonly paper: string` (`STYLES[spec.dress].paper`). `ribbonResultFor` computes the layout once and hands it to `itineraryRows`, which today lays it out a second time. Both transports spread `ribbonResultFor` (`answerRibbon` in `src/site/explorer/worker.ts`, `runInline` in `src/site/explorer/worker-client.ts`), so neither changes.

**Interruptions (the policy the ruling leaves to the builder):**

- **A lean** (a press on an itinerary row) mid-reveal ends it at once: the whole plate is under the Glass as it flies, since leaning onto a stretch still bare would show the reader a road not yet drawn.
- **A picked journey** mid-reveal ends it at the press (the draw's own `retire()`), the old plate standing whole while the surveyor works, and the new plate arrives whole (ruled: no reveal on a picker change).
- **Turn about** mid-reveal freezes the reveal where it stands (`hold`) until the turned plate arrives, which then reveals from bare paper (ruled: Turn about plays it). Ending it at the press instead, as a picked journey does, flashed the old plate whole for one frame before the turned plate's bare paper on the spike's first build (finding 9).
- **A draw that fails** retires any reveal it held, leaving the plate on screen whole.
- **The reader's own camera** (the wheel, a drag, a pinch, the Glass's presses and keys) and **a refit** (a resize, the slip folded) leave it running: the clip is on the plate inside the transform target and in percentages of the plate's own box, so it rides every pan, zoom and refit (re-baseline note 4).
- **Reduced motion switched on mid-reveal** collapses the rest of it on the next frame (the blanket, live). **Print** mid-reveal prints the whole plate (above).

### Alternatives rejected

- **Covers** (a paper-coloured box over each strip, shrinking upward): the ruling names a clip on the plate, the hover lift moves the plate and not a cover (finding 6), and RB11 holds the transform target's one face (`same(s.map, ["IMG#rb-plate.plate"])` in `e2e/suites/ribbon/room.ts`).
- **One `<img>` per strip, each clipped to its own box:** the same RB11 face, one more decode a strip, and the lift misregisters them.
- **`element.animate()` from script:** the reduced-motion blanket reaches CSS only (re-baseline correction 3), so a script animation owes a live preference gate of its own, and the ruling names a CSS reveal.
- **Inline SVG, the road drawn by `stroke-dashoffset`** (the issue's option 2): set aside by the ruling.
- **A keyframe set per strip count, or one registered property per strip:** one registered number with a `clamp()` per hole serves every count from 3 to 7 (finding 3).
- **A transform wipe (a scale on the plate):** the house lift writes `transform` (finding 6).
- **The polygon in an inline custom property gated by the class:** the house puts per-element values on the element as the property itself (`handbook/specs/ui-design.md`, "Per-element data never travels through a custom property"), and the retire clears the inline clip in the same call that removes the class.
- **A fill mode on the run:** a filled clip would cut the hover's shadow after the run (above).
- **Reduced motion left to the blanket alone** (the spike's first build): one frame of bare paper before the whole plate (finding 9). The blanket stays the answer for a reveal already running.
- **Turn about ending the reveal at its press,** as a picked journey does: one frame of the old plate whole before the turned plate's bare paper (finding 9).
- **No ground** (the plate cut out over the desk): the hidden strips would show the walnut desk through holes in the paper.
- **A fixed delay for the room's landing:** the plate's arrival moves with the worker (finding 7); the reveal waits on the stage's own running animations instead.
- **Hiding only the drawing inside each strip's cloth, or only the road's middle column:** ruled out by Q1; measured on the spike, they left 46 and 54 percent of the last strip's marks standing at the start, the compass rings' edges and the sea's edge among them (finding 9).
