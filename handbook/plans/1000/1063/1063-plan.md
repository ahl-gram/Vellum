### What the section says

The lead states the principle for every test, in the house's prescriptive voice with Alex's citation (conventions: state the rule, keep the citation tag, drop the story), says the mechanism is item 11's skip-list entry for a flaky and a slow check alike (ruling D4), and says the section sets no rule for a unit test (call C4). Then seven bullets:

- **Flaky** is items 11 and 12 unchanged (ruling D3): a flaky step is pulled out when its flake issue is filed, at its third counted CI row at the latest, and its fix is tried before that row or while it is out.
- **Too slow** is the ruled rule (D1): at most two claims nothing else holds, on at most two pages or rooms, and 10 seconds or more; with the unit counted (call C5).
- **What a step holds** and **what it costs**: the counting method below.
- **What a cut buys**: only a cut that shortens the slowest lane saves wall clock, and a step counts whatever its lane (ruling D6).
- **One honest fix** for a slow step: one planned change aimed at its own seconds, timed before and after, and a pull only if it is still too slow (ruling D2).
- **Who finds a slow step**: whoever builds a list, and every pull request that adds or lengthens a browser check (ruling D5).

The counting method, written so a reader can apply it by command, was measured on real steps (the five exemplars below and the cut list's 36 pieces):

- **The unit is the step** (call C5), the unit of the skip list: a step that prints several checks is measured and counted together; a check outside every step is counted alone, as the one-check step item 11 says it is wrapped in before it can be skipped; and a step or check whose result or page state a later check reads is counted together with those readers, because item 11 has each reader set up for itself what the pulled one left, so pulling the first alone saves nothing. Of the cut list's 13 thin pieces of 10 s or more, 7 run outside every step today (RD1, H13f, H13d, H16, H15a, H15c, L1h: `git grep -n -E 'step\("(RD1|H13f|H13d|H16|H15a|H15c|L1h)[",( ]' -- e2e/suites` prints nothing), and P28 and P31 sit inside step `P28 to P33` (`e2e/suites/cards.ts`), which runs six checks. Four of the seven feed later checks: `rd1Ladder`'s ladder is read by RD2 and by step `RD3, RD4` (`e2e/suites/region-detail.ts`, whose comment says RD1, RD2 and the ladder are deliberately not stepped); `h13fNoScriptRead` returns both H13f's and H13h's reads (`e2e/suites/home.ts`); H15a's wait for the drift is what H15b's wheel stops, and H15c's wait for it to start again is what H15d's flight stops (`e2e/suites/home/drift.ts`).
- **What a step holds**: its claims (each a rule it can fail on alone, read from its checks' sentences and confirmed in its code); the pages, rooms and windows it reads them in; and, per claim, whether another browser check or a unit test fails when that claim breaks. The holder search is `git grep` for the selector, symbol or constant the claim turns on, then a read of each hit. It errs toward finding false holders (a mention is not an assertion), which is why each hit is read; and a unit test of the arithmetic behind a claim, or of the helper that measures it, does not hold the page's claim (`squeezes` in `test/e2e/corners.test.ts` tests CO3's measuring helper, not the page).
- **What a step costs**: the time from the last result line before the unit to the unit's own last result line, read off the timestamps GitHub puts on every log line, the median of three lane runs. The harness prints no step's seconds (`makeStep` in `e2e/support/step.ts` keeps no clock; only `per-suite wall clock` is printed, in `e2e/support/suites.ts`). Blind spots, each erring high: a step's first result also carries the navigation before it, and a suite's first step carries the suite's boot. Three runs is a small sample, and runner speed varies (Z25 read 22.2 s in one run, 35.1 and 35.5 in the other two).
- **What a cut buys**: a run waits on its longest job, so a cut from a lane that is not the slowest saves nothing until the lanes are re-shared (Issue #1007's lesson at lane level), and a cut from the slowest lane saves only down to the next one. `test/e2e/lane-timings.test.ts` caps each lane at 0.3 of the total measured seconds (`BALANCE_CAP`); it sets no lower bound, so it forces a re-share only when a cut pushes some other lane past the cap. Ruling D6 counts a step's cost whatever its lane, on the measured ground that cuts across the lanes together reach further than one lane's (below).
