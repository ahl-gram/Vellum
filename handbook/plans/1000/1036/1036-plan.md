## The design

`rejectBridges(coarse, immovable, fine, seaLevel)` keeps its signature, its size checks, its gained mask and its four-connected components. What changes is what it does to a component that touches two or more coarse landmasses (`touched.size >= 2`):

1. **The cut.** It sinks only the fewest cells of that component whose loss leaves no four-connected run of the rest touching two coarse landmasses. Four-connected, because `labelLandmasses` and `shoresTouched` are, and the no-fusion guards read landmasses that way.
2. **The order and the tie-break.** The shores a component touches are parted one at a time. Each shore's contact is the number of the component's cells four-adjacent to that landmass. The shores are taken in order of contact, least first, ties by landmass id (the id `labelLandmasses` gives the coarse field), and each in turn is parted from every shore after it in that order. Each parting takes a minimum vertex cut between the cells touching that shore and the cells touching any later shore, inside the cells still standing, and among equally narrow cuts takes the one nearest the shore being parted: the source side of the minimal minimum cut, which is unique whatever augmenting paths found the flow. A cell touching both sides is always in the cut. So the patch stays with the shore it hugs most, and the cut falls where the patch is narrowest against the shore it barely touches.
3. **The depth.** Each cut cell is set to `seaLevel - max(n + e, e + s, s + w, w + n) - BRIDGE_REJECT_EPS`, where `n`, `e`, `s` and `w` are its four edge neighbours' heights above the waterline (`max(0, value - seaLevel)`), counted 0 for a neighbour off the grid or itself cut. In every square of four cells holding a cut cell, the square's two land corners in a saddle are that cell's two edge neighbours in the square, so its average stays at or under the waterline and the drawn coast (`marchingSquares`, the cell-centre average) cannot bridge the cut. The heights are read from `fine`, the field the rule was given, and every cut is known before any depth is set.
4. **Everything else stays.** A gained piece the cuts leave touching no shore is kept as an islet, as the rule already keeps new islets. Cells the world chart holds (`immovable`) are never gained, so never cut. Coarse land is never touched. Each pass of the chain (`chainFromBare` in `src/world/detail-chain.ts`, one pass per ancestor, nearest first) only lowers cells, so a later pass cannot undo an earlier cut, and a depth set by an earlier pass still holds when a later pass lowers a neighbour.

What it guarantees, the same as today and no more: after the rule, no four-connected component of the remaining gained cells touches two coarse landmasses of that ancestor. What it adds: the drawn coast does not bridge any cut cell.

**Alternatives rejected, measured on the spike:**

- **A cut dipped just under the waterline (`seaLevel - BRIDGE_REJECT_EPS`, as today's rule sinks).** The drawn coast passes through the cut cells' own points, so the strait has no width and the shores read as joined: 24 drawn-fusing saddles on the lake window at band 5 against today's 4 (`arms-lake.log`, arm `neck-source`). `out/1036-probe/render/strip-lake-shore-42--b5--arms.png` shows the arms left to right at 1:1 on the 1500 px sheet: today, this hairline cut, the highest-neighbour depth, the design, the widened cut.
- **A cut dug as deep as its highest neighbour stands.** Clears the lake (0 drawn fuses) but fails the constructed shallow-water saddle in T2 below (`out/1036-probe/fixtures-check.ts`, run with `ARM=neck-source-mirror`), because the diagonal water corner can sit just under the waterline.
- **A cut widened by one cell on each side.** Where the widening reaches coarse land it bares that coarse edge again, a fresh straight run (the strip's fifth panel).
- **The cut nearest the shore the patch hugs most** (the sink side of the cut). More nubs and specks along the strait (`out/1036-probe/render/wide-b5-sinkpair.png` against `wide-b5-pair.png`) and twice the drawn fuses at the hairline depth (48 against 24).
- **A cut weighted by how high the ground stands**, so it follows the lowest pass. Not the ruled "narrowest neck", and it needs a weighted max-flow for no measured gain.
- **Roughening or tapering the floor's gate, or smoothing at draw time.** Ruled out by Alex (comment 6452075084) and by the epic's non-goals.

## Files

**`src/terrain/bridge-cut.ts`** (new, about 130 lines). Which cells to cut; no constant, no field.

- `export function neckCut(cells: ReadonlyArray<number>, coarseIds: Int32Array, w: number, h: number): number[]`. `cells`: one gained component's grid indices, ascending. `coarseIds`: `labelLandmasses(coarse, seaLevel).ids`. Returns the grid indices to sink, in the order the partings found them, each parting's cells by ascending local index. Body: `touch = cells.map((i) => shoresOf(i, coarseIds, w, h))`; `order = partingOrder(touch)`; `live = new Uint8Array(cells.length).fill(1)`; for `t` from 0 to `order.length - 2`: `cut = isolate(cells, live, touch, order[t], new Set(order.slice(t + 1)), w, h)`, then for each `k` of `cut` set `live[k] = 0` and push `cells[k]`.
- `const NEIGHBOURS: ReadonlyArray<readonly [number, number]> = [[1, 0], [-1, 0], [0, 1], [0, -1]]`.
- `function shoresOf(i: number, coarseIds: Int32Array, w: number, h: number): number[]`: the distinct ids other than -1 of the cell's in-grid four neighbours in `coarseIds`.
- `function partingOrder(touch: ReadonlyArray<ReadonlyArray<number>>): number[]`: every id in `touch`, by contact (how many entries of `touch` hold it) ascending, then id ascending.
- `type FlowNet = { addEdge(from: number, to: number, capacity: number): void; saturate(source: number, sink: number): void; reachable(source: number): Uint8Array }` and `function createFlowNet(nodes: number): FlowNet`. Edges live in arrays the closure owns (`head: Int32Array` filled with -1, and `to`, `cap`, `next` number arrays), each `addEdge` pushing the edge and its reverse of capacity 0 at the next even and odd index, so an edge's reverse is `e ^ 1`. `saturate` repeats a breadth-first search from `source` over edges with `cap > 0`, recording the edge into each node, and pushes the path's smallest capacity along it (subtract on each edge, add on its reverse), until `sink` is unreached. `reachable` is one more such search, returning 1 for every node reached. All mutation is of the closure's own arrays, never a parameter's (`no-param-reassign` with `props: true` is on).
- `function wireCutNet(cells: ReadonlyArray<number>, live: Uint8Array, touch: ReadonlyArray<ReadonlyArray<number>>, shore: number, others: ReadonlySet<number>, w: number, h: number): FlowNet`: nodes `2k` and `2k + 1` for local cell `k`, the source `2m`, the sink `2m + 1`, `m = cells.length`, so `createFlowNet(2 * m + 2)`; `INF = m + 1`. For each live `k`: `addEdge(2k, 2k + 1, 1)`; `addEdge(2m, 2k, INF)` if `touch[k]` holds `shore`; `addEdge(2k + 1, 2m + 1, INF)` if `touch[k]` holds any of `others`; and `addEdge(2k + 1, 2j, INF)` for each in-grid four-neighbour that is a live cell `j` of this component (an `Int32Array(w * h)` filled with -1 maps grid index to local index). It reads `live` and never writes it.
- `function isolate(cells: ReadonlyArray<number>, live: Uint8Array, touch: ReadonlyArray<ReadonlyArray<number>>, shore: number, others: ReadonlySet<number>, w: number, h: number): number[]`: local indices of the parting's cut, ascending. `net = wireCutNet(...)` with the same arguments, `net.saturate(2m, 2m + 1)`, `reach = net.reachable(2m)`, and return every live `k` with `reach[2k] === 1 && reach[2k + 1] !== 1`.

## Calls made

Each is mine, on the rule given; Alex may overrule any.

1. **"Narrowest" is the fewest cells, four-connected** (the minimum vertex cut), not a fixed width and not the lowest ground. Rule: the ruling's words, and the guards' own connectivity.
2. **The cut is dug, not dipped.** Depth is the highest sum of two consecutive edge neighbours' heights over the waterline, plus `BRIDGE_REJECT_EPS`. Rule: a hairline cut draws no strait at all (24 drawn fuses on the lake at band 5), and the single-neighbour depth fails T2.
3. **Ties go to the cut nearest the shore being parted, and the shore the patch touches least is parted first.** Rule: the patch stays with the shore it hugs, which keeps most of the ruled "rest of the patch as shore"; the other side drew more specks on the lake.
4. **Three or more shores are parted one at a time against all later ones.** Rule: every pair must be parted (the guarantee), and this is the simplest deterministic order that does it.
5. **A leftover piece touching no shore is kept as an islet.** Rule: the bridge rule already keeps new islets (T4's islet); and they are rare and tiny (31 pieces of at most 2 cells over 572 close-ups, `strands.log`), so sinking them would add a rule for nothing seen. The visible specks are a coarser survey's own land, which no version of the rule may take.
6. **The dug cells may sit below the nautical shoal line and the topographic deep bands** (a quarter of cut cells below the shoal line). Rule: the no-bridge guarantee needs the depth; capping it at a render style's threshold would tie the terrain to a drawing constant and reopen the drawn bridges; drawn, the effect is a faint mottling inside a one-cell strait (the stills under "How deep the cuts go").
7. **The guard's bound is 56 on four witnesses** (the lake at band 5 and the sweep's three worst walls), not the issue's 20 town close-ups. Rule: the ruling keeps the floor's town runs, which reach 42 and 40 and are untouched by this change; the bound is the sweep's worst under the cut (50) plus 6 cells, and every witness reads 63 or more under today's rule.
8. **The two old #397 tests that pinned whole sinking are rewritten in place** (T4, T5), and T6 keeps its title. Rule: their titles state the behaviour Alex ruled away.
9. **`scripts/region-detail/partition.ts` keeps calling `rejectBridges`** and its doc line is corrected; no copy of the old rule is kept for it. Rule: no production code is kept only for a measuring script; the PR body names the moved numbers.
10. **The rulebook records the ratification as given, for the bridge rule alone**, in the wording under Rosters and doctrine, and `region-and-voyage.md` gains the rule as a bullet beside the bridge guarantee it refines.
