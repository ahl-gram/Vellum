## The design (pull request 1, as D1 a rules)

### The doors wait on the page's own clock, run fast

H13d (a dead bundle under reduced motion still reveals its doors) and H13f (scripts off under reduced motion never reveals them) are claims about the cascade at the reveal's 10 s mark, and both wait out the wall clock to reach it. The fix runs the page's document timeline ten times fast through the debugger's own animation control, then waits for that timeline, not the wall, to pass the mark, then reads the door once. Every CSS animation on the page, the reveal included, runs on that timeline; scripts are not involved, so the scripts-off arm reads the same.

Measured on this Mac (`out/1068-dry-run/doors.ts` and `out/1068-dry-run/doors-after.ts`, logs beside them, at ecd425f0):

- `Animation.setPlaybackRate` with rate 10, sent **after** the navigation, reads back 10 on the new document, runs `document.timeline.currentTime` ten times fast (20063 page ms at 2020 ms of `performance.now()`), and reveals the doors at about 1.0 to 1.1 s of wall where today's control took 10.15 s.
- Sent **before** the navigation it does not outlive it: the new document reads rate 1 and reveals at 10.16 s. So the rate is set after each navigation, and it never leaks into a later document.
- It holds with scripts off (the H13f arm read the same 20 s of page time in 2.0 s of wall).
- The reveal's animation started 141 to 931 page ms after the document's origin across the probes.
- Every probe ran on Brave, the first browser `findBrowser` finds on this Mac. CI drives Google Chrome, which this Mac does not have (`ls "/Applications/Google Chrome.app/Contents/MacOS/Google Chrome"` fails), so the rate's behaviour on CI's browser is UNVERIFIABLE until the first push carrying it. Both are Chromium, whose debugger's animation control this is, and the wait is written so a rate that did not take fails by name (a settle timeout naming `h13d-past-the-reveal` or `h13f-past-the-reveal`) rather than passing slowly: stop condition 1 catches it.

The wait reads `{ t, start }`, the timeline's time and the door's reveal animation's start where it has one, through `makeSettle` with `tolerant: true` (the read may cross the navigation), and settles once `t` is at least 20 000 page ms, twice the ruled delay (call C5). It throws on its budget, inside the unit's step (clause 4). Its predicate carries no claim (clause 6); the door is read once, after it.

H13d's bundle block moves into a `finally`, since the wait can now throw. H13d gets a step of its own and H13f with H13h one together, as item 11 asks of a check outside every step.
