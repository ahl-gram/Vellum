## The design

**The desk wait departs on what the room's layout writes.** `fitRoom` in `src/site/shared/room.ts` is the one writer of `#sheet`'s inline `width` and `height`; the stylesheet moves the sheet's place with the window at once and never its size, and the only other thing that changes the sheet's rect size is the fold's slide (`createSheetSlide` in `src/site/shared/sheet-slide.ts`, a translate and scale), which no fold at the desk step starts. So the first wait in `atTheDesk` asks that the sheet's size has left the harness window's, through a new `sameSize` beside `sameSheet` in `e2e/suites/reading-room/reads.ts`, and keeps its stillness (two equal reads) and `atDesk`. At the two windows the suite uses the sizes differ by 84 px of width (824.59 against 740.27), and a room that never lays out at 1440 by 900 still times out by the wait's own label. This is the form the settle doctrine gives for "a wait for a room laid out at a new window" ("departs on something the room's layout writes for that window"), and it is the check's rewrite for an explained red, carrying the marker `vellum-implementer` reads (Files R3).

**The unit fixture holds it.** `deskRun` in `test/e2e/kit-waits.test.ts` already claims, by its first test's name, that the desk is read "never at the window's size alone or at a stray layout", but its `wide` stand-in (the window changed, the layout not yet run) keeps the harness sheet's place, which the stylesheet never does, so it could not red today's departure. `wide` becomes the re-placed sheet the probe read, and the file's two desk-half tests red on today's code and pass on the planned wait.
