## The design

**Deletion is a second way out of the run, beside the pull.** The section is renamed "When a check
leaves the run" and opens by naming both ways: pulled out under an `E2E_SKIPS` entry and a restore
issue while flaky or too slow (unchanged), or deleted with no entry and no restore where its coverage
is needless. Four new bullets close the section, after "Who finds a slow step":

1. **The two grounds**, in Alex's words with the issue's examples: no critical journey and easy to
   verify by hand (a simple page; the masthead every page wears); or coverage a critical journey
   does not need (one rule in every room from one shared module, one layout across many windows,
   one claim by several paths, or a claim whose logic a unit test holds and whose path another
   check reads, leaving only the page's wiring). Being red, flaky or slow is never a ground by
   itself.
2. **Who rules**: every deletion is Alex's, by name, at the plan's stop; narrowing is a deletion of
   the reads dropped, and what a narrowed check keeps is proven as a new guard.
3. **Move or list**: each claim moves to a unit test where one can hold it alone, else it is listed
   as lost coverage on the ruling issue and in the pull request body.
4. **Clean-up**: the deleting pull request greps every mention and edits each live one, keeps dated
   records, gives flake rows the disposition *deleted*, removes the `E2E_SKIPS` entry and closes its
   issue in the same pull request, and takes an emptied suite out of its rosters.

**Where the move-to-unit-test rule lives**: `handbook/specs/check-placement.md`, since it is a rule
about which instrument holds a claim; settle-doctrine points at it. One normative home each
(`handbook/specs/conventions.md`, Where a rule lives).
