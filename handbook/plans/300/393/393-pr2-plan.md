## The design

**A written world travels as its sentence, and every room founds it again on arrival** (ruling 1). The address keys are the Writing Room's, `found` and `v`, read by the one `readWritten` lifted into the shared folder, so no second reading of `v` exists. A room handed a written link founds it through the same founder the Writing Room uses (the name index fetched only for a shaped sentence, installed only on `NAMES_CHECKSUM`), and draws `f.seed` with `recipeOverrides(f)`: the founding's own overrides plus `foundingText` when a word was recognised. So the chart in every room carries the written world's naming tongue and letters its sentence, and no room reads a chart number or a setting beside the sentence.

**What a written link carries beside the sentence** (ruling 3). Dress and time keys apply wherever a room reads them today (`style`, `theme`, `legend`, `arms`, `beasts`, the camera `cx`, `cy`, `k`, `survey`, `year`, `table`, and the rooms' own `i`, `a`, `b`). Identity keys (`seed`, `type`, `band`, `land`, `coast`, `outline`, `looseness`, `islands`, `isleSize`, `raggedness`) are never written beside a sentence, and a crafted link carrying both draws the sentence's world. A sentence the founding refuses (a version this build lacks, an over-long or unreadable sentence, a name index that cannot be read) reads as absent, as any malformed key does under Issue #192: the room draws the rest of its address. The Writing Room keeps reading only `found` and `v`, as PR #1163 built it.

**The road from the Writing Room.** The legend row gains a gold road after the plate press: "Explore it in / The Explorer" (ruling 8's words), `id="wr-explorer"`. Before any world stands its `href` is `../explorer/`; after each drawn founding it is `../explorer/` + `writtenHash(f.sentence, f.version)`, with `&theme=vegetation` appended while the vegetation plate is laid on, so the Explorer opens on what the room shows (`explorer-from-road-*.png`). The row stays on one line at 1024 and 1440 (M5).

**The Explorer.** On arrival it founds the address's sentence (`readAddress` in `src/site/explorer/hash-sync.ts`), holds the written world (`src/site/explorer/written-hold.ts`), and seats every control from it through the ordinary keys (`identityParams`), so the seed box shows the chart number and the Broadside shows the written world's kind of land, climate, shape, count, sea level and coast. While the sentence holds the world, each draw takes the founding's own overrides (`draftOverrides`), never the controls: 4 of the 18 founded sea levels in the founding sample fall between the sea-level slider's steps (M2), and the tongue has no control, so drawing from the controls would draw another world. The address it writes is the sentence, its version and the dress (`writtenParams` inside `writeHash`).

**The Explorer's Land panel holds while the sentence stands** (ruling 2). Every row the panel shows, the type and the climate now among them, stands in the panel's held box under the note "the sentence holds the land; to change it, write it anew in the Writing Room", and refuses a press as the held rows do today (`seatOf` and `refuses`). The Hand (style, theme, legend, arms, beasts, survey), the camera, the Glass, the regions, the verso, the Chart Table and every road keep working. A new chart number, typed in the seed box with Enter or thrown by the dice, lets the sentence go: the new number is drawn with the settings the panel showed, as the Explorer keeps them for any new number today, and its own tongue and no lettering (WR10's release reading: `#seed=42&type=archipelago&band=polar`). Pressing Draw on the same number redraws the written world. The type and climate become rows of the Land panel (`.l-row` wrappers, `display: contents`, so nothing moves on a plain world; SEAT reading M6).

**The other rooms.** The Print Room and the Reading Room found the address's sentence at boot, put its chart number in their seed box, draw it while the seed box holds that number, write it back (their hash writers and their roads, through `writtenParams`), and let it go when a draw of another chart number lands. The Prospect and the Ribbon found the sentence at boot and draw it; they pass their hash on verbatim as they do today, so the sentence rides their roads unchanged.

**The Chart Table carries a written world's tongue, never its sentence** (call C5). A sheet filed from a written world (a survey from the Glass, a prospect from the card or the Prospect page) carries `culture-<id>` beside its other world fields; two tongues of one chart number are two worlds on the table; a tongue the house does not know drops the sheet. Region sheets and prospect plates never letter the sentence (`engravedLines` in `src/render/layers/cartouche-founding.ts` returns nothing for a region world; a prospect plate's cartouche names its town), so the sheet loses nothing the plate draws.

**What the Explorer shows of a written world** is the chart itself: the cartouche letters the sentence (`explorer-held-flagship-1024-cartouche.png`), the chart folio carries the founded title and chart number, and the held box's note says the sentence holds the land, its closing words the road back (below). Nothing else is added to the Explorer (call C14).

**The road back to the Writing Room** (ruling 4: on the Explorer, only while a sentence holds the world; ruling 5: the held note's closing words). The held note's closing words are the road: the note reads "the sentence holds the land; to change it, " followed by the link "write it anew in the Writing Room" (`#land-held-road`, the slip's own link ink, underlined, 9.89:1 on the held box's cream), whose `href` is `/writing-room/` + `writtenHash(sentence, version)`, so it opens the Writing Room with the sentence in its line, ready to be rewritten. It exists only in the written seat: a new chart number takes it away with the hold. It carries the sentence and its version and nothing else, since the Writing Room reads nothing else (ruling 3). The press row and the chart are exactly as today at every width (`q5-note-road-1024.png`, `q5-note-road-1440.png`: chart 505.2 by 390 and 832.5 by 642.7 px, the row on one line, the slip body unscrolled).

The orchestrator's condition, answered from measurement (M10 to M12, stills in `out/393-pr2-road/`): the "24 px into the chart's corner caption" this plan's first menu reported compared the legend row's box with the chart folio's box. On the house's own rule, the legend clear of the folio's written lines (BR12 in `e2e/suites/broadside/dress.ts`), no arrangement of a fourth road meets the folio's lettering at 1024, 1280 or 1440: the row's seat keeps the lettering 32 px clear wherever the row stands beside it, and at 1024 the row rises above the folio. What a road in the press row does cost is the chart's room while a sentence holds the world: the row needs 590.2 px for one line (551.8 with Issue #1159's stand-in tighter presses) and is given at most 533.7, so it wraps to two lines, and the stage gives the second line its height. The chart shrinks from 505.2 by 390 to 473 by 365.1 px at 1024 by 768, and from 832.5 by 642.7 to 752.7 by 581 at 1440 by 900; at 1280 by 800 these worlds' rows already take two lines today, so nothing moves there. A shorter verb ("Rewrite in") narrows nothing, since the room line "The Writing Room" is the widest line; the road's place in the row (first or last) changes nothing. So the follow-up menu offered the road in the row and the note's own words, which cost nothing, and ruling 5 chose the note's words. The first menu's figure is corrected here and in M9: no road touches the corner caption's lettering.

**Rejected alternatives**, so none is rediscovered mid-build:
- *Carrying the founded settings instead of the sentence* (ruling 1 chose against it): a hand-edited link could letter any words over any world, so the cartouche would stop being proof that its words founded the chart; every room's grammar would learn a tongue key and a sentence key; links grow long.
- *A `culture` key in the room addresses*: only the let-go-and-keep-the-names option ruling 2 passed over needed one; no address carries a tongue.
- *Drawing a written world from the Explorer's controls once seated*: draws another world for 4 of 18 sea levels and drops the tongue (M2).
- *Founding in the worker*: every room already founds on the main thread in the Writing Room's way, and the founding needs the page's installed name index.
- *Five copies of the founding call, one per room*: one `foundAddress` and one `writtenParams` serve every room (call C2).
- *Letting a room release a written world before its new draw lands* (Print Room, Reading Room): a failed draw would leave the held world on screen with an address naming another (call C9).
- *Disabling the held selects*: the house's held rows refuse a press rather than being disabled (`handbook/specs/ui-design.md`, the Broadside's Land panel), so a keyboard reader still reaches them and hears why.
- *New browser checks in each room's own suite*: one part file in the writing-room suite holds the whole carry (call C10).
- *The road as a press in the row under the chart*, "Write it anew in / The Writing Room" (ruling 5 passed over it): the row wraps to two lines and the chart gives up room while a sentence holds the world (M10, M11); a shorter verb and the road first in the row narrow nothing (M10).
- *Issue #1159's tighter presses as the fix for a road in the row*: they bring one line to 551.8 px, still more than the 533.7 px the widest window gives (M10).
