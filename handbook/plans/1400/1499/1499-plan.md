## What this builds

At every standing bird's-eye plate whose key is full, the sea has a line in the key. Today `composeBirdseye` (`src/prospect/birdseye/compose.ts`) cuts the key's drafts at `KEY_ENTRIES = 8` (`src/prospect/birdseye/names.ts`), and `planKey` puts the landmarks and the gates before the sea and the river, so a place with eight or more of its own entries loses the sea's line. Where the water also holds no clear seat for the name (`seaNameAt` in `src/prospect/dress/compose-c.ts`), ruling D6 (a) of Issue #755 ("where no clear open water holds the sea's name, it is left to the key", comment 7203257230) is broken: the key does not hold it either. After this change the key holds the sea at every standing and every ruined harbour plate; before the founding no plate has a key (ruling 2 of comment 7204904697), which this does not reopen.

And BW3 in `test/prospect/birdseye-world.test.ts`, which today skips an unseated sea with `continue`, asserts the other half of D6: where no water holds the sea's name, the plate's key names it.

## The design

### The rule

**The key's drafts keep their order** (`planKey`: the keep, the church, the market, the bridge or bridges, the quay, the gates in the plan's order, then the sea at a harbour place, then the river under the town), are filtered by era as today (a standing plate keeps every draft, a ruin its bridge and its world names, a plate before its founding has no key), and are then cut to the key by one function, `withinKey`, in place of today's plain cut at eight. The sea becomes a kind of draft of its own (`of: "sea"`), so `withinKey` finds it by kind, never by its label; the river stays `of: "world"`.

**Option (a), recommended: the last gate gives way.** `withinKey` keeps the first eight drafts, except that where the sea falls past them it takes the eighth line: the first seven drafts, then the sea. The key never holds more than eight lines. Each of the 21 places carries at most one keep, church, market, bridge line and quay, five landmarks (`census.log`, column `kinds=`), so the seven kept include every landmark there and what gives way is a gate, the last of them; its number leaves the picture with it (the number tags are the key's own, `keyTags` over `geometry.key`), while the gate itself is still drawn. The river is not given a line it does not have today. At every place whose own entries number seven or fewer nothing changes, and no plate before its founding or in ruin changes (a ruin's key is at most its bridge, the sea and the river).

At Laukuwelua (seed 42 index 0) the key becomes: 1. The Keep, 2. The Great Church, 3. The Market, 4. The Bridges, 5. The Quay, 6. The Pong Gate, 7. The Noloatatani Gate, 8. The Great Woaku (today 8. is The Wuwatau Gate). `arms-census.log`, the row `42 0 Laukuwelua capital standing antique a`.

### Alternatives rejected

- **The sea's line only where the water holds no seat for the name** (the 17, not the 21). The seat reads the key's own boxes (`seaNameAt` avoids `boxesOf(key)`, the key panel and its number tags), so a key that waited on the seat would have to be decided in the dress, after the composer, and `geometry.key` would stop being the key the plate draws. And 908 of the 929 standing harbour plates already name the sea in the key whether or not the water holds it; the four whose water holds it (Melo, Orinea, Mariolis, Sou) would be the only ones that do not. Planner's call 1.
- **A gate whose town the picture already names gives way first.** BW2 (`test/prospect/birdseye-world.test.ts`, "a gate is named for the first town its road reaches") pairs the i-th gate entry with the i-th distinct gate that reaches a town, so dropping a gate from the middle shifts the pairing and costs that test a rework, and a place where no such gate exists needs a second rule beneath it. Every option on the menu drops gates from the end, which keeps BW2 as it is.
- **The key's cap raised to nine for every plate.** It would still leave the sea out at the five places whose own entries number nine or ten (Melo 10; Faloridell, Dra, Thro and Laukuwelua 9, `census.log`), and would give the river a ninth line, one panel row taller, at the 35 places of option (c) whose own entries number seven; option (b) is the ninth line given to the sea alone, and option (c) brings the river back without a taller panel.
- **The cap wins and D6's words are read again** (the review's second suggestion on PR #1498): ruled out by the issue itself, "Give the sea an entry in a full key, so a name the water cannot hold always appears in the key" (Alex's ruling, comment 7206986078).

  assert.ok(
    stenernfjord.input.harbor && eraFor(stenernfjord.input, stenernfjord.year) === "standing",
    "seed 29 index 8 is a standing harbour place",
  );
  let named = 0;
  let keyed = 0;
  let ruins = 0;
  for (const p of [...SEEDS.flatMap((seed) => places(seed)), stenernfjord]) {
    if (!p.input.harbor) continue;
    const era = eraFor(p.input, p.year);
    const { boxes, geometry, key } = engraveBirdseye(p.w, p.input, STYLES.antique, p.year);
    const sea = plateSurroundings(p.w, p.index, p.year).seaName;
    const where = `seed ${p.input.seed} index ${p.index}`;
    assert.ok(sea !== null && key.some((k) => k.label === sea), `${where}: the key does not name the sea`);
    if (era === "ruined") ruins++;
