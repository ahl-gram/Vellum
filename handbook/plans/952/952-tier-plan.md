## The design

**A new tier of two kinds.** `SettlementKind` in `src/society/sites.ts` gains `"farmstead"` and `"mill"`. The type checker then names every record and mapping keyed by kind (measured by widening the union in the planner's tree and running `npm run check`, then restoring it: 8 errors in `src/atlas/compose.ts`, `src/itinerary/dress/strip.ts`, `src/prospect/input.ts`, `src/render/layers/settlements.ts` (three) and `src/render/place-card.ts`). The sites the checker cannot see are listed under Files, each changed or left with its reason.

**Where they grow.** On every window no larger than `FARMSTEAD_MAX_WINDOW_UV` (1/32, band 5's size), appended after the hamlets in `regionPeople`. Nothing draws a window that small until Issue #954 adds band 5, so before then the tier is reached by the engine and the tests alone.

**What decides a farmstead: the world alone.** A lattice one world cell apart (`FARMSTEAD_LATTICE_WORLD_CELLS` 1; the round's half-cell points joining at band 6 are past the ruled depth). Each point `(ix, iy)` takes the fork `farmstead:${ix},${iy}` off `createRng(seed)` and draws, in this order, its roll, its jitter in x and its jitter in y; the spot is `((ix + 0.5 + (ju - 0.5) * 0.7), (iy + 0.5 + (jv - 0.5) * 0.7))` world cells. The point holds a farmstead when `hamletSite` scores the world cell `(Math.round(x), Math.round(y))` of its spot `(x, y)`, as `hamletAt` rounds a hamlet's (outside `EDGE_MARGIN`, land, neither snow nor alpine, no higher than the hamlets' elevation band), `roll < FARMSTEAD_DENSITY * score`, it stands no nearer than `FARMSTEAD_SPACING_WORLD_CELLS` (0.45) to any world settlement or to the spot of the hamlet candidate, if any, in the hamlet lattice cell holding the point (`hamletSpotAt` below), and its name draw (`hamletName(r.fork("name"), culture, worldNameSet(world))`) returns one. That one hamlet cell covers the spacing: a hamlet's jitter keeps its spot at least (1 - 0.7) / 2 x 5 = 0.75 world cells inside its 5-cell lattice cell, more than the 0.45 spacing, so no hamlet in a neighbouring cell can stand that near (measured: checking the eight neighbouring cells too changes no candidate over the whole of chart 42). Its founding year is the hamlets' rule on the point's own fork, `world.title.year - 8 - r.fork("age").int(Math.max(1, Math.min(240, world.title.year - 16)))`. Every test is in world space, so a farmstead's existence, its world-space spot, its name and its founding year never depend on the window (its drawn cell is that spot snapped to the close-up's land, as a hamlet's is): the round's defect (2 of 18 band-5 marks lost at band 6, because its spacing read the window's own marks) cannot happen.

**What makes a mill: the river the close-up draws (M9).** A farmstead whose snapped cell lies within `MILL_REACH_WORLD_CELLS * cpw` region cells (0.18 world cells, the round's reach; `cpw` the close-up's region cells per world cell) of a river cell of the close-up (`riverCells`, after Issue #951 the laid rivers) is a mill, and stays where the farmstead stood (it is not moved onto the bank). Its name in the data stays the farmstead's name; the sheet labels it `${name} Mill`, as the ruled stills drew it, so its place card reads "Lokai, Mill" and glosses the name in the local speech. The mill always stands on a river the reader sees. Because Issue #951 lays each river afresh for each window, whether a farmstead is a mill could differ between two band-5 windows that overlap, near their edges, when the reader pans; no deeper band shows the tier within the ruled depth. Measured at `8031db65` with the tier as planned: the 20 band-5 windows of the census towns grow 166 marks, 5 of them mills (8.3 marks and 0.25 mills a sheet); over the 35 band-5 windows of the label probe (the census towns and the first four non-village towns of seeds 42, 7, 2, 15 and 23) 315 marks, 9.0 a sheet; of the 302 farmsteads each also seen from the window one Glass step (an eighth of the window) east or south, none is a mill in one and not the other.

**Placing it.** The candidate's spot is projected into the window and snapped with `snapToLand` at `landSnapRadius`, as hamlets are; one with no land in reach is dropped. A farmstead or mill that lands on or beside a laid road stays there (M3); no road departs from or arrives at one, since the roads are laid before the tier is placed and `layWorldRoads` finds its towns through `placed.regionIdxOf` by world index, which no tier mark has. Measured at `8031db65`: of the 166 census marks, none stands on a laid road cell, 5 stand within one cell of one, and none of those is within a world cell of a harbour town whose road M10 bends. The 5 are drawn as ruled over the heavier close-up roads in `out/952/pass/tier-near-roads.png` (`node out/952/mock/tier-near.ts`): each reads as a roadside farm. The tier's spacing is measured from each world settlement's place in the world, and a harbour town Issue #953 seats on the close-up's shore is drawn up to `HARBOUR_REACH_WORLD_CELLS` (about 2.1 world cells, `src/world/seat-settlements.ts`) from it, so a farm can be drawn nearer than 0.45 to it: over the 35 band-5 sheets, 3 of 315 marks are (Toanui 0.16 world cells from Laulume on chart 7, Vlermeksk 0.13 from Radin on chart 15, Vyditsa 0.44 from Lyrkol on chart 2; `out/952/measure/near-towns-summary.txt`, drawn as ruled in `out/952/near-towns/near-towns.png` by `node out/952/mock/tier-near-towns.ts`). Spacing from the drawn dot instead would make a farm's existence depend on the window, which Sub 0's fix forbids, so they stand where they fall, as a roadside farm does under M3; the one whose mark lands on the town's name is M11's.

**Drawing it.** Region sheets only, by construction: `generateWorld` never places one. The marks are M5's, exactly as the ruled stills drew them, in the style's `ink` and `labelHalo`, at `k = widthPx / 1500`:
- **Farmstead: a little house in profile, standing on its point** like the castle (the plan-view dot convention does not apply to a profile mark; `handbook/specs/rulebook.md`, Mixed projections). With `s = 1.15 * k`: one `path`, `M(px - 2.4s, py) L(px - 2.4s, py - 2.6s) L(px, py - 4.8s) L(px + 2.4s, py - 2.6s) L(px + 2.4s, py) Z`, filled `ink`, stroked `labelHalo` at `0.7 * k`, `stroke-linejoin` round.
- **Mill: a spoked wheel.** A `g` holding a circle at `(px, py)`, radius `3.2 * k`, filled `labelHalo`, stroked `ink` at `1 * k`, then one path of four diameters of that circle, at 0, 45, 90 and 135 degrees (unit vectors `(1, 0)`, `(Math.SQRT1_2, Math.SQRT1_2)`, `(0, 1)`, `(-Math.SQRT1_2, Math.SQRT1_2)`), stroked `ink` at `0.7 * k`.

**Naming it (M7): every mark is labelled, and its name is the first word a crowded sheet drops.** Each farmstead and mill is labelled at the hamlet's size (13 on a region sheet), with the hamlet's label gap, and never force-placed. Its label claims the arena after the feature labels (the seas', lakes', forests' and realms' names) and before the sea beasts: the settlements layer is built in two phases, every other settlement claiming before the feature labels as today and the farmsteads and mills after them. A label layer's evaluation order is its priority (`labelledLayers` in `src/render/map-renderer.ts`), so claiming inside the settlements layer after the hamlets, as the stills were drawn, would let a farm's name evict a sea's. Measured at `764853bb` over the 35 band-5 windows: claiming with the settlements, 299 of the 315 marks are named and 1 sheet loses a feature word (the lake name "The Great Woaku" on the sheet of Paukilua, chart 42); claiming after the feature labels, 298 are named and no sheet loses one. On the three sheets the stills were ruled from (Won and the capital of chart 42, Wutoanu of chart 7) the two orders draw byte-identical sheets (`out/952/measure/stills-order-summary.txt`). Both orders on the Paukilua sheet, as ruled: `out/952/labels/claim-order.png`. The tier's groups stay last in `layer-settlements`, in the order they hold today (their rank is last), so `lod-controller.ts`, `draw-ceremony.ts` and every reader of group order or `data-name` see the same layer; a world sheet, which has no tier, draws byte-identically.

**Keeping its mark clear of words (M11: the names step around the farm houses).** No label avoids a mark today, since only words claim the arena. Without a reservation, 5 of the 315 marks over the 35 band-5 sheets are printed under a word: Toanui under the name of the harbour town Laulume seated beside it (chart 7), Nel under the end of the town name Tlecuapec 0.47 world cells away (chart 23), and three under a realm's name (Densyitsa on chart 15, Sonrydsk on chart 15, Tziachin on chart 23); the 32 hamlet dots on the same sheets are under none. The settlements layer's first phase therefore claims each farmstead's and mill's mark box in the arena before any settlement's label is placed, so every word placed by search after it (the settlements' names, the feature words and the tier's own names) steps around the marks. Two placements do not search the arena and stay as they are: a capital's, seat's or town's name that finds no free candidate is forced into its first one (`placeSettlementLabel`), and a realm's name that finds none is forced onto its centroid (`placeRealmLabel` in `src/render/layers/realm-label-placement.ts`); either could still cover a mark, and on the 35 sheets neither did. And a river's name, set along its river, searches the arena but may graze any claim up to `RIVER_MAX_OVERLAP` (`handbook/specs/chart-dress.md`, "A river keeps its name over a graze"), a mark's box included. Measured with the reservation (`out/952/measure/options-summary.txt`): no mark under a word; two town names (Laulume, Tlecuapec) and three realm names take another spot; no word is lost; 300 of the 315 marks are named against 298; the three sheets the stills were ruled from draw byte-identically (`out/952/measure/stills-reserve-summary.txt`). The five sheets under each option Alex was shown, the third the one he ruled: `out/952/near-towns/options.png`.

**Keying it.** Legend rows "Farmstead" and "Mill" appear only on a sheet that has one, after "Hamlet". Their key icons are drawn by `settlementGlyph`; the farmstead's is lowered `3 * k` like the castle's and the ruin's, since it stands on its point (the house's body centre is 2.76k above its point, so lowered 3k it sits 0.24k below the row's centre), and the mill's wheel, centred on its point, is not.
